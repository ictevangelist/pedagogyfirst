Chapter Three

03Feedback

Feedback is too often treated as another word for marking. It isn’t. Good feedback gives pupils information they can use to improve what they do next, and that is a far higher bar than writing a comment in the margin.

Rooted in the work of Hattie & Timperley, Sadler, Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick and others

What Feedback Is

01 🔍Feedback Is Not Marking

If a student never reads or acts on your comments, you have marked but not given feedback. Before returning work, plan how students will engage with it. No engagement, no feedback.

Find this under Make feedback more useful

02 🎯Feed Up, Feed Back, Feed Forward

Every comment should answer three questions: where am I going, how am I doing, what next? Most feedback answers only the second. Check yours covers all three before you hand it back.

Suggested technology structured feedback template tool · Informed by Hattie & Timperley

Find this under Make feedback more useful

03 📈Four Levels of Feedback

Before writing a comment, ask: am I addressing the task, the process or the person? Task and process feedback improve learning. Self-level praise undermines it. Name your target.

Suggested technology feedback checklist or rubric tool · Informed by Hattie & Timperley

Find this under Make feedback more useful

04 ⚠️One Third Makes It Worse

Over a third of feedback interventions decrease performance. Before giving feedback, check: does this comment address the work or the student? If it is about the student, rewrite it.

Suggested technology feedback audit or review tool · Informed by Kluger & DeNisi

05 📜Sadler's Three Conditions

Share the criteria before students begin. Show them how to compare their work against it. Give them time to act on what they find. Without all three steps, feedback cannot function.

Suggested technology success criteria display tool · Informed by Sadler

Timing & Frequency

06 ⏰Immediate for Simple Tasks

For simple or procedural tasks, correct errors immediately. Shute found quick feedback prevents mistakes from embedding. Do not wait until next lesson to fix what can be fixed now.

Suggested technology auto-marked quiz or response tool · Informed by Shute

07 🕐Delayed for Complex Tasks

For complex work, a short delay before feedback lets the learner process first. Immediate correction on difficult tasks can short-circuit thinking. Let the struggle do its work.

Suggested technology LMS with scheduled feedback release · Informed by Shute; Kulhavy

08 🔄During, Not Just After

Feedback given while students are still working is formative. Feedback returned weeks later is a post-mortem. Move around the room, intervene in the moment. Proximity is strategy.

Suggested technology live collaboration or monitoring tool · Informed by Wiliam; Black & Wiliam

09 📋Comment Only, No Grades

Butler found comments alone produced the greatest gains. A grade cancels the comment: students see the score and skip the words. If grades are mandated, delay until feedback is used.

Suggested technology annotation tool; hide grades option · Informed by Butler

Find this under Make feedback more useful

10 📊Reduce Volume, Raise Focus

A page of comments overwhelms. One or two precise targets land. Identify the one thing that would make the biggest difference right now and focus entirely on that.

Suggested technology digital highlight or pin tool · Informed by Shute; Wiliam

Source & Direction

11 👥Peer Feedback with Structure

Give students criteria and a protocol before they assess each other's work. Topping showed peer feedback improves learning for both, but without scaffolding it becomes empty praise.

Suggested technology peer review tool with criteria prompts · Informed by Topping

12 🔬Self-Assessment as Process

Ask students to assess their work against criteria before you mark it. Andrade found the act of comparing, not the accuracy of the judgment, improves performance. Process is the point.

Suggested technology self-review checklist tool · Informed by Andrade

Find this under Build independence · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

13 💬Dialogic Feedback

Build reply loops into feedback. Let students question and clarify comments over time rather than receive a final judgment. Carless showed uptake rises when feedback is a conversation.

Suggested technology threaded comment or chat tool · Informed by Carless

Find this under Help pupils act on feedback

14 🤖Technology as Feedback Source

Use automated tools for factual accuracy and procedural correctness; reserve your time for voice, argument and creativity. Match the feedback source to what is being assessed.

Suggested technology adaptive or AI-assisted feedback tool · Informed by Shute; Black & Wiliam

15 📋Whole Class Feedback

Read the set and identify patterns: common strengths, recurring errors, one priority next step. Teach to the gap, not to each paper. Sustainable for teachers; precise for students.

Suggested technology shared display or document tool · Informed by Wiliam; Hattie

Feedback & the Learner

16 🧠Task, Not Person

Feedback that threatens self-concept decreases performance. "This paragraph needs restructuring" works. "You need to try harder" does not. Direct feedback at the work, not the person.

Suggested technology annotation tool focused on work · Informed by Kluger & DeNisi

Find this under Make feedback more useful · Inclusive practice: feedback and next steps

17 💡Feedback Must Cause Thinking

Write "why might this be stronger with evidence?" not insert the evidence yourself. If feedback needs no effort from the learner, it is information, not feedback. Thinking is the point.

Suggested technology annotation tool with reply function · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Help pupils act on feedback

18 🔔Praise the Strategy, Not the Trait

Butler found task-involving feedback improves performance. Ego-involving feedback shifts attention from learning to self-image. Praise what the student did, not who the student is.

Suggested technology feedback phrasing guide or template · Informed by Butler; Kluger & DeNisi

19 🌱Build Feedback Literacy

Teach students how to read and act on feedback before you give it. Model with an exemplar: here is the comment, what it means, what to do next. Feedback literacy is taught, not assumed.

Suggested technology worked example or modelling tool · Informed by Carless; Boud

20 💪Seven Principles of Good Practice

Audit your practice against Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's seven principles. Do you clarify criteria? Enable self-assessment? Encourage dialogue? Close the gap? Redesign where you do not.

Suggested technology feedback design checklist tool · Informed by Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick

Closing the Gap

21 ⏱️Build In Time to Act

Feedback with no time to act on it is wasted. Begin the next lesson with students reading and responding to comments before anything new. The response is the learning event.

22 ❓Questions, Not Corrections

Return work with questions, not corrections. "What evidence supports this claim?" demands more thinking than rewriting it for them. The cognitive work belongs with the learner.

23 📄Sustainable Feedback

Boud argues feedback should build the learner's capacity to judge quality. If students always need you to tell them how they did, the system has failed. Design for independence.

Suggested technology self-assessment or portfolio tool · Informed by Boud

Find this under Build independence · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

24 🛠️Feedback as Design, Not Event

Plan feedback into your scheme of work: who gives it, when, in what form, and when students act on it. Feedback designed into learning from the start works. Left to chance, it does not.

Suggested technology curriculum planning or LMS tool · Informed by Boud; Sadler; Carless

The thinking

Feedback is not what the teacher gives. It is what the learner does with it.

Hattie and Timperley’s work showed us that feedback can have a powerful effect on achievement, but it is not automatically useful. Some feedback helps, some does very little, and some can make things worse.

Sadler’s work found the important bit that is often missed. Feedback only works when learners understand what quality looks like, can compare their work against it, and know how to close the gap. Nicol and Macfarlane Dick took this further by placing feedback within self-regulation, helping pupils become better at judging and improving their own work.

Butler’s study is a useful warning too. When grades sit alongside comments, pupils often look at the grade and miss the guidance.

In practice

Technology can make feedback faster. That only matters if pupils use it.

Digital annotation, audio comments and shared documents can shorten the gap between work and response to almost nothing. That matters. Feedback given while the work is still fresh has a far better chance of shaping what pupils do next than feedback returned two weeks later.

Used well, the same tools also make whole-class feedback more manageable. You can spot common patterns, address them quickly, and avoid writing the same comment thirty times.

The trap is automated feedback that gives a score, a tick, or a badge and stops there. That may be useful information, but it is ultimately unhelpful. Feedback earns its keep when pupils have time to think, improve, redraft, correct, compare or try again.

The win is not faster feedback. The win is better work because pupils had time to use it.

Further reading and evidence

Where this thinking comes from

The wider evidence around this area, kept deliberately short. No single study validates all 24 strategies; these are the traditions the thinking comes from, and good places to read further. How this site uses evidence.

  • Hattie & Timperley (2007), The Power of Feedback, Review of Educational Research. The feed up, feed back, feed forward model and the four levels of feedback.
  • Kluger & DeNisi (1996), The Effects of Feedback Interventions on Performance, Psychological Bulletin. The meta-analysis behind the sobering finding that over a third of feedback interventions made performance worse.
  • Butler (1988), Task-involving and ego-involving properties of evaluation, BJEP. Comments alone beat grades, and grades cancel comments. A small study with a long shadow; its pattern has replicated in spirit if not always in size.
  • EEF (2021), Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning. The guidance report: six recommendations, including laying foundations before feedback and planning for how pupils use it.

Use this for professional learning

A 30 minute activity for your team

Is the effort you spend marking coming back as learning?. A 30 minute activity for a department, phase or professional learning group, using this guide as it stands.

  1. Read the thinking section above together.
  2. Estimate, honestly, the hours your team spent on written feedback last fortnight, and what students did with it.
  3. Explore the strategies and pick two or three that shift effort from writing feedback to acting on it.
  4. Discuss the mechanism: why does feedback only work when it causes thinking?
  5. Consider context: subjects with extended writing, practical work, your marking policy as it stands.
  6. Ask whether technology genuinely helps here, audio comments perhaps, or whether it just moves the work.
  7. Choose one change to trial across one class set.
  8. Agree how you'll judge it: the quality of student responses to feedback, not the volume of feedback given.

The model behind this, and how to run it across a school or trust, is on the professional learning page.
