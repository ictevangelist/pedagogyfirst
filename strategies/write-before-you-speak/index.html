Write Before You Speak

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

08 📝Write Before You Speak

Ask students to write before any verbal response is taken. Writing prevents the quickest from foreclosing others’ thinking. The pen democratises the room before the voice takes over.

Suggested technology shared doc, whiteboard or response tool · Informed by Tobin; Rowe

Find this under Increase participation · Make thinking visible · Support access and participation · Inclusive practice: access and participation · Try this tomorrow

Part of Wait Time & Thinking Space in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

In open discussion the quickest thinkers set the terms before anyone else has finished forming a view, so a short burst of silent writing before any voice is heard gives every pupil a position of their own to speak from.

The card's phrase makes the argument for it: the pen democratises the room before the voice takes over. On top of this, it leaves you with thirty written answers to glance at instead of three spoken ones to remember.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Before a discussion on whether the experiment's results support the hypothesis, the class gets ninety seconds of silent writing. You then open the discussion by asking a mid-attaining pupil to read out what they wrote, rather than improvising something new on the spot.

Try it

A way to begin

Try one discussion question tomorrow with ninety seconds of writing first, and take the first contribution from the page rather than from the hand.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Who contributes after writing who wouldn't have contributed before.
  • The difference in quality between answers read from the page and answers improvised on the spot.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Keep the writing short and rough, because the moment it becomes a task to complete it stops being thinking time and starts being work to mark.
  • It pairs really naturally with think, pair, share, so pupils write alone, test it on a partner, and then speak.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 👥 Think-Pair-Share Lyman’s think-pair-share gives every student thinking time before public exposure. Pairs test ideas with a peer first. The result is more considered and more substantive discussion. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • ✍️ Mini Whiteboards All students write their response and hold it up simultaneously. The teacher reads the room in seconds: full participation, no waiting, no dominant voices. Works across all subjects. 02 Formative Assessment · Elicit Evidence of Learning
  • ⏰ Wait Time 1: After You Ask Rowe found most teachers wait under one second before speaking. Extending to three seconds increases the length and complexity of responses. Silence is thinking, not a gap to fill. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • 🌎 Every Voice, Every Time Eliciting evidence only works if every student can respond. Offer written, verbal, visual and digital means of expression. The method you choose decides who gets to show what they know. 02 Formative Assessment · Elicit Evidence of Learning
