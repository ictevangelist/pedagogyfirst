The Generation Effect

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

14 ❓The Generation Effect

Slamecka and Graf found generating an answer, even a wrong one, before being told it produces stronger retention than passive exposure. The effort of generation drives encoding, not the accuracy.

Suggested technology response tool; pre-teach quiz · Informed by Slamecka & Graf; McDaniel

Part of Desirable Difficulties in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Slamecka and Graf found that generating an answer before being told it, even a wrong answer, produces stronger retention than passive exposure does. The wrong answers part is worth sitting with for a moment, because it's the effort of coming up with an attempt that does the good, not whether the attempt was right.

In practice, this is the finding that makes it worth asking before telling. Whenever the answer arrives before pupils have had a go at one of their own, that useful effort simply never happens, and it costs almost nothing to let it happen.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Introducing a new French verb, you show the infinitive and ask the class to guess the first-person form before revealing it. Half the guesses are wrong, but every pupil now has an attempt of their own to compare the answer against, and that comparison is where the learning gets its grip.

Try it

A way to begin

Build one ask-before-tell moment into an explanation you already give, so pose the question, take some guesses, and then reveal. It only costs you a few seconds, and you can use it almost anywhere.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils will actually commit to a guess. A class that's been trained to wait for the answer will need convincing that wrong attempts are genuinely welcome, so it can take a little while to bed in.
  • What the wrong guesses tell you about how pupils are thinking, because they're useful evidence for you as well as useful effort for them.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The attempt has to come before the answer, not instead of it. Generation works because the correction follows, and leaving errors uncorrected is a different activity with different risks.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 📈 Test Before You Teach Quiz students before teaching. Struggling to retrieve primes the brain; students who fail before instruction remember the answer more reliably once taught. Failure is productive. 01 Retrieval Practice · The Testing Effect
  • 🧔 Embrace the Struggle Bjork coined desirable difficulties for conditions that slow apparent learning but improve long-term retention. Effortful retrieval is the clearest example. Resist the urge to make it easier. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties
  • ✅ Provide Instant Feedback Metcalfe shows instant feedback is essential for confident errors. For general retrieval, allow a processing gap before revealing answers. Speed matters most when confidence is highest. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • 💡 Student Generated Questions Ask students to write questions that could appear in an assessment on this topic. Collate the best and use them for review. Generating questions demands higher order thinking than simply answering them. 02 Formative Assessment · Students as Learning Resources

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Slamecka and Graf named the generation effect in the 1970s, and McDaniel's work has since carried it into more realistic educational materials. The card’s attribution stands as published: Slamecka & Graf; McDaniel.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Retrieval Practice.
