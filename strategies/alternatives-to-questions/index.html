05 🗣Alternatives to Questions

Dillon found statements and silence elicit richer responses than questions. Try: Tell me more. I had not considered that. Not every probe needs to end with a question mark.

Suggested technology discussion or annotation tool · Informed by Dillon

Find this under Ask better questions

Part of The Quality of Questions in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Dillon's finding runs against instinct, because statements and silence often draw out richer responses than questions do. A steady stream of questions can turn a discussion into an interrogation, with your voice doing all the driving and every exchange routed through the front.

Saying tell me more, or I had not considered that, hands the floor back without demanding a particular answer, and the card's own summary is the one to remember: not every probe needs to end with a question mark.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Picture a discussion of a moral question in RE, where a pupil offers a view. You simply say, "I had not considered that", and stop there. The pupil keeps going, and then a second pupil responds to the first one rather than waiting for the next question to arrive.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What comes after a statement compared with what comes after a question.
  • How many questions you ask in ten minutes of discussion, because just counting them is revealing in itself.
  • Whether pupils start responding to each other when you probe less.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Silence is one of Dillon's alternatives too, which makes wait time the natural companion to this card.
  • Statements only really work when they're genuine, so I had not considered that only lands if you actually hadn't.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • ⏰ Wait Time 1: After You Ask Rowe found most teachers wait under one second before speaking. Extending to three seconds increases the length and complexity of responses. Silence is thinking, not a gap to fill. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • ▶️ Break the IRE Pattern IRE (Initiation, Response, Evaluation): ask, answer, judge. Alexander shows this pattern limits thinking. Replace evaluation with a follow-up question. What makes you say that? 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 🔁 Build on Student Responses Alexander’s EEF programme showed gains when teachers extended rather than evaluated. Return an answer: Aisha says X. Does anyone want to add or challenge? Conductor, not judge. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • ❓ Open Questions Demand More Dillon found genuine open questions, where the teacher does not know the answer, produce the most extended student reasoning. Not every question needs a predetermined destination. 04 Questioning & Discussion · The Quality of Questions
