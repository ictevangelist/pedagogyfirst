05 🔀Hinge Questions

A hinge question is a diagnostic question at a lesson turning point. Wrong answers each map to a known misconception. Responses tell you whether to move on or go back. Never improvise it.

Suggested technology polling or quiz tool · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Check understanding

Part of Elicit Evidence of Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Every lesson has a turning point where you decide, usually on a feeling, whether the class is ready to move on. A hinge question swaps that feeling for evidence, with one diagnostic question, placed at the hinge, answered by everyone.

What makes it a hinge question rather than just a question is the way the wrong answers are designed. Each one maps to a misconception you already know pupils hold, so the spread of responses tells you not just how many are stuck but why they're stuck, and that's the information you can actually teach from.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Planning a lesson on adding fractions, you write one question for the midpoint, with four responses: the right answer, the classic add-tops-and-bottoms error, a common denominator slip, and a simplification mistake. Every pupil answers at once on whiteboards, and the spread across the room decides the second half of the lesson.

Try it

A way to begin

Plan a single hinge question for one lesson next week. Write the wrong answers first, from the errors you already see in books, and then build the question around them.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which distractor gets chosen, because that's your diagnosis, and it's much more useful to you than the count of correct answers.
  • What you actually did next. As the guide has it, evidence has done nothing until it changes what happens in the following five minutes.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The card's warning really does deserve its own line: never improvise it. An improvised hinge question just collapses into a recall check, and the diagnostic power goes with it.
  • It needs an all-pupil response method to work, because a hinge answered by three volunteers is a hinge in name only.
  • One good hinge per lesson is plenty. The cost is planning time, which is exactly why the wrong answers are worth engineering carefully once and then reusing.

Technology second

Where technology fits

A polling or quiz tool, the approved one in your setting, can get you the whole room's answer at the hinge in seconds, with the spread visible straight away. The important thing is that the tool has done nothing until what it shows changes your next five minutes.

The expensive part of a hinge question is drafting distractors that map to real misconceptions, and AI, through the approved tool in your setting, can draft them for you to judge. A STAIR-shaped prompt looks like this:

Swap the role, topic and year group for your own, and then do the part only you can do, which is checking each distractor against the misconceptions your pupils actually hold, and going back and forth until they match. That's the I in STAIR, and it's the bit the diagnostic power depends on.
