05 🔀Hinge Questions

A hinge question is a diagnostic question at a lesson turning point. Wrong answers each map to a known misconception. Responses tell you whether to move on or go back. Never improvise it.

Suggested technology polling or quiz tool · Informed by Wiliam

Part of Elicit Evidence of Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Every lesson has a turning point where you decide, usually on a feeling, whether the class is ready to move on. A hinge question replaces the feeling with evidence: one diagnostic question, placed at the hinge, answered by everyone.

What makes it a hinge question rather than just a question is the design of the wrong answers. Each one maps to a misconception you already know pupils hold, so the spread of responses tells you not just how many are stuck but why.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A maths teacher planning a lesson on adding fractions writes one question for the midpoint, with four responses: the right answer, the classic add-tops-and-bottoms error, a common denominator slip, and a simplification mistake. Every pupil answers at once on whiteboards. The room's spread decides the second half of the lesson.

Try it

A way to begin

Plan a single hinge question for one lesson next week. Write the wrong answers first, from the errors you already see in books, then build the question around them.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which distractor gets chosen. That is the diagnosis, and it is more useful than the count of correct answers.
  • What you actually did next. As the guide has it, evidence has done nothing until it changes what happens in the following five minutes.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The card's warning deserves its own line: never improvise it. An improvised hinge question collapses into a recall check with no diagnostic power.
  • It needs an all-pupil response method to work; a hinge answered by three volunteers is a hinge in name only.
  • One good hinge per lesson is plenty. The cost is planning time, which is exactly why the wrong answers are worth engineering carefully once and reusing.
