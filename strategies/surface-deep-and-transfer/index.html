Surface, Deep and Transfer

02 📈Surface, Deep and Transfer

Hattie and Zierer distinguish surface (what), deep (why) and transfer questions. Each serves a different stage. Reaching transfer before surface is secure sets students up to fail.

Suggested technology discussion prompt or slide display tool · Informed by Hattie & Zierer

Find this under Ask better questions

Part of The Quality of Questions in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Not every good question suits every moment, and Hattie and Zierer make a really helpful distinction here. Surface questions establish what, deep questions probe why, and transfer questions ask where else this applies, and each one serves a different stage of learning.

The thing that tends to go wrong is reaching for transfer too early. If you ask students to apply a concept elsewhere before the surface knowledge is secure, you're setting them up to fail, and that failure can look like inability when it's really just a matter of asking too soon.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Early in a unit on electricity you might ask what the components of this circuit are. Once that's secure, you can ask why the bulb dims when a second one is added. Later still, you can ask where else the idea of sharing a limited supply turns up. It's the same topic all the way through, but three stages and three different kinds of question.

Try it

A way to begin

Before writing the key questions for your next lesson, ask yourself one question first, which is what stage this class is at with this content.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How students handle why questions, because struggle there often tells you the surface layer isn't secure yet.
  • Whether the discussion changes when the question matches the stage the class is actually at, rather than the stage the scheme of work says they should be at.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Stage is about this class and this content right now, not about ability. The same lesson can hold students at different stages, and only you can see where each one is.
  • Surface questions aren't lesser questions. The previous card makes the same point about recall, and the two frameworks agree with each other.
