Embrace the Struggle

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

11 🧔Embrace the Struggle

Bjork coined desirable difficulties for conditions that slow apparent learning but improve long-term retention. Effortful retrieval is the clearest example. Resist the urge to make it easier.

Suggested technology any retrieval tool, hints disabled · Informed by R. Bjork

Part of Desirable Difficulties in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

This is the idea underneath the whole retrieval guide, so it is worth meeting on its own terms. The conditions that make learning feel hard and slow in the moment, spacing it out, mixing it up, testing instead of reviewing, are often the very conditions that make it last. The conditions that feel smooth and productive mostly produce confidence without retention.

That runs against every instinct in a helpful profession. When a pupil struggles to retrieve, the natural move is to make it easier: a hint, a prompt, a quicker reveal. Bjork's work says the struggle is doing the useful work, provided what is being asked for was genuinely taught.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A teacher running a retrieval quiz turns the hints off and lengthens the silence before revealing answers. Pupils grumble that it feels harder than last term's version. Weeks later, the harder version is the one they remember.

Try it

A way to begin

Take one retrieval activity you already run and remove one support: the hints, the word bank, or the notes-open first pass. Change nothing else, so you can see what the added difficulty does.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • The gap between how the activity feels and what it produces. Pupils rating it harder while recalling more, later, is the effect working as described.
  • Confident wrong answers. Difficulty exposes the illusion of knowing in a way smooth practice never does.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Desirable difficulty is not the same as confusion. Difficulty from an unclear task or untaught content is just a barrier; the struggle has to be in the retrieving, not in working out what is being asked.
  • Secure the foundations first. Effortful retrieval works on knowledge that exists, which is why the guide pairs this idea with checking that the basics are in place before pushing beyond them.
  • How long to let the struggle run is not a rule anyone can hand you. That is a judgement made in the room, by the teacher who knows these students, watching whether the effort is still productive.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🚫 Remove the Scaffolding Retrieval with notes open is reading, not retrieval. Fade support gradually: notes first, then headings only, then nothing. The transition to unaided retrieval is where consolidation happens. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties
  • ⏱️ Forget Before You Retrieve Do not retrieve immediately after teaching. Wait a day, then quiz. Wait a week, then quiz again. The gap between learning and retrieval is where consolidation happens. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties
  • 🔬 Teach the Illusion of Knowing Show students their own illusion of knowing. Ask them to rate confidence before revealing the answer. When they were certain and wrong, that is the precise moment to intervene. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • ❓ The Generation Effect Slamecka and Graf found generating an answer, even a wrong one, before being told it produces stronger retention than passive exposure. The effort of generation drives encoding, not the accuracy. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties

Technology second

Where technology fits

The card's tech line says it all: any retrieval tool, hints disabled. If the approved tool in your setting values speed, streaks or fastest-finger reveals, it's removing the desirable difficulty that makes the practice worthwhile. Retrieval is not a game show.

Where the evidence sits
