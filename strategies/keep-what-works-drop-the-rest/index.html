Keep What Works, Drop the Rest

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

19 🧹Keep What Works, Drop the Rest

Winne: learners refine an approach by checking whether it worked. Have students trial a study method, judge it against results, and keep only what the evidence supports.

Suggested technology method log with outcomes · Informed by Winne

Part of Strategy Knowledge & Use in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Pupils keep study methods the way anyone keeps habits, because they're familiar and they feel productive, not because they've actually checked, and re-reading survives on exactly that. Winne's finding is that learners refine an approach by checking whether it worked, which means the checking has to be arranged.

So the idea is that pupils trial a method, judge it against their results, and keep only what the evidence supports, so they're testing their own studying in the same way they'd test anything else.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

For three weeks, pupils log one line before each weekly quiz saying which method they used to prepare. Afterwards they add the score. At the end they read their own log, and one pupil finds that the evenings spent recopying notes went with their lowest scores, and the ten-minute self-quiz with their highest. Evidence they collected themselves tends to persuade far better than anything you could have told them.

Try it

A way to begin

Attach a one-line method log to a quiz routine you already run. The routine is already there, so the logging is the only new bit.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Comfort methods that the log shows aren't doing much. Watching a pupil meet that evidence is the strategy doing its work.
  • Whether anyone actually drops a method. Keeping the winner is easy, but dropping the familiar loser is the hard part, and it's the part worth praising.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • This needs a regular assessment rhythm to judge against, because without one there's no evidence for the log to collect.
  • Single data points mislead, so judge across weeks rather than after one quiz, because one bad Friday doesn't really tell you much about a method.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Winne describes self-regulated learners as running a feedback loop over their own methods, evaluating each one against outcomes and updating as they go, and this strategy builds that loop where pupils can see it. The card’s attribution stands as published: Winne.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Metacognition & Self-Regulation.
