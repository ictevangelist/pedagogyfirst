Pre-Train the Vocabulary

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

18 📖Pre-Train the Vocabulary

Mayer’s pre-training: teach key terms before the main explanation. A vocabulary slide or pre-lesson video builds familiarity first. Learners who know the words can focus on the ideas.

Suggested technology vocabulary slide; pre-lesson video · Informed by Mayer

Find this under Explain difficult ideas · Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Support access and participation · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand · Inclusive practice: language and explanation

Part of Multimedia & Dual Coding in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

An explanation delivered in unfamiliar terms is really two lessons squeezed into one, because pupils are learning the words and the ideas at the same time, and working memory has to carry both. Mayer's pre-training principle separates them, so you teach the key terms first and then the ideas.

Learners who already know the words can give the explanation their full attention, and as the card puts it, they can focus on the ideas.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Before a lesson on electrical circuits, pupils meet current, voltage and resistance on a vocabulary slide, or in a short pre-lesson video, with a picture for each one. When your main explanation uses the terms, they label something that's already half-known rather than something brand new.

Try it

A way to begin

List the terms your next explanation can't be given without, and teach the unfamiliar ones before it starts rather than pausing to define them mid-flow.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether the questions during the explanation are about the words or about the ideas.
  • Whether the pre-trained terms start showing up in pupils' own talk and writing.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • How many terms need pre-training, and which ones, is a judgement about this class, and you're the one who knows who's meeting each word for the first time.
  • Pre-training pairs really naturally with connecting to prior knowledge, because one prepares the words and the other prepares the ideas they'll attach to.

Where this connects

  • 🔗 Connect to Prior Knowledge Rosenshine: connect to prior knowledge before introducing anything new. Use a retrieval starter or concept map on screen to activate what students know. Link before you launch. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Structuring Explanations
  • 📦 Concrete Before Abstract Willingham: anchor new ideas to concrete examples first. Use images, video or objects to make the abstract visible before naming it. Move to the abstract once the concrete is secure. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Structuring Explanations

Technology second

Where technology fits

A vocabulary slide or a short pre-lesson video, made with the approved tools in your setting, puts the terms in front of pupils before the explanation needs them, and that's really all this takes.

Drafting a tight vocabulary list with pupil-friendly definitions is the sort of job AI does really well, through the approved tool in your setting:

You know which terms your class already holds, so cut and swap until the list really is the six that open up the explanation. Iteration is essential, and the definitions need to match how you'll use the terms in the room.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Pre-training is one of Mayer's multimedia principles, and its logic is pure cognitive load theory, which is to reduce what's novel at the moment of explanation. The card’s attribution stands as published: Mayer.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Explanations & Modelling.
