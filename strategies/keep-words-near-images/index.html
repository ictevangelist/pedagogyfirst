05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding

Keep Words Near Images

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

15 📍Keep Words Near Images

Mayer’s spatial contiguity: place words near the images they describe. Labels sit beside the diagram, not in a separate list. Use slide design to keep related information together.

Suggested technology integrated slide layout · Informed by Mayer

Find this under Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Support access and participation · Inclusive practice: language and explanation

Part of Multimedia & Dual Coding in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Each stretch of screen between a label and the thing it names has to be crossed by the eye and bridged by memory. Mayer's spatial contiguity principle keeps words next to the images they describe, so nothing needs holding during the journey.

A numbered key below a diagram is the classic offender: accurate, complete, and in the wrong place.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Laying out a slide on the parts of a flower, a teacher places each label beside its structure with a short connecting line, rather than listing the names in a panel down the side. Reading the slide and reading the diagram become the same act.

Try it

A way to begin

Scan the resources for your next topic for separated captions, keys and label lists, and move each word next to the thing it names.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How long it takes pupils to find a named part on the diagram.
  • Whether questions about the diagram use the labels' vocabulary.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The principle applies wherever words and images share a surface: worksheets, wall displays and textbook pages, not just slides.
  • For a resource you cannot redesign, annotating it live under a visualiser puts the words where they belong.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 👀 Avoid Split Attention Sweller’s split attention: separate text and diagrams force learners to search between them. Integrate labels directly into the image. Use annotation tools to build the diagram live. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 🖼️ Words and Images Together Paivio’s dual coding: the brain processes words and images through separate channels. Design every slide with both: a key visual paired with minimal text. Two routes to the same idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
  • ➡️ Signal What Matters Mayer’s signalling: bold, arrows and highlights direct attention to what matters. Use annotation tools to signal live during the explanation. Without it, learners search alone. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • ❌ Remove the Clutter Mayer’s coherence: remove every word, image and sound that does not support learning. Strip clip art from slides. Cut background music. Every element on screen must earn its place. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Spatial contiguity is one of Mayer's multimedia principles and shares its mechanism with Sweller's split attention effect. The card’s attribution stands as published: Mayer.
