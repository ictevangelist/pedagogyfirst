Four Slots, No More

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

07 🧠Four Slots, No More

Sweller: working memory holds about four items. Every screen element competes for those slots. Strip slides to essentials. Press B to blank the screen when you want attention on you.

Suggested technology B/W key; clean slide design · Informed by Sweller

Find this under Explain difficult ideas · Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand

Part of Managing Cognitive Load in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Working memory holds around four items at a time, and every element on your screen takes up one of those slots. A logo, a border or a sentence nobody needs right now is taking up space the explanation really requires.

Sweller's point is about how the mind works rather than how slides look. Stripping slides back to the essentials is looking after your pupils' working memory, and so is pressing B to blank the screen when you want the attention on you.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Preparing to explain supply and demand, you cut the slide down to one graph and a four-word heading. When the discussion moves beyond the graph, the B key blanks the screen and the class's four slots can go to the voice doing the explaining.

Try it

A way to begin

Count the elements on each slide of the deck you'll teach next. Anything beyond the essentials is a candidate for deletion, and the B key covers the moments when the essential thing is the explanation itself.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where pupils look during the explanation, both with a full screen and with a blank one.
  • Whether recall improves on the ideas you taught from stripped-back slides. If nothing changes, have a look at what else is loading the moment.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • What counts as essential depends on the idea and the class, so the four-item limit is general but the editing is yours to do.
  • A blank screen shifts the whole load onto your spoken explanation, which is why this pairs so well with small steps.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🔇 Say It or Show It, Not Both Sweller’s redundancy effect: narrating text on screen forces double processing. Say it or show it, not both. Use the B or W key to blank the slide while you talk through the idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • ❌ Remove the Clutter Mayer’s coherence: remove every word, image and sound that does not support learning. Strip clip art from slides. Cut background music. Every element on screen must earn its place. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • ✂️ Chunk and Reveal Mayer’s segmenting: break content into chunks. Use slide animations to reveal one idea per click, not auto-play. Let working memory process before the next chunk arrives. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 👀 Avoid Split Attention Sweller’s split attention: separate text and diagrams force learners to search between them. Integrate labels directly into the image. Use annotation tools to build the diagram live. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits
