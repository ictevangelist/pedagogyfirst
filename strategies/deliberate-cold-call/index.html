Deliberate Cold Call

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

07 🎙️Deliberate Cold Call

Select who answers deliberately, not at random. Track participation so nobody stays invisible across lessons. Where appropriate, target the students whose thinking you most need to hear.

Suggested technology participation tracking tool · Informed by Wiliam, Black

Find this under Increase participation · Inclusive practice: participation and response

Part of Elicit Evidence of Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Hands up tends to sample the confident few and leave everyone else unheard. Random selection is fairer, but it's still chance, and chance can leave the same pupils unheard for weeks. Choosing who answers on purpose lets you reach the thinking you most need to hear, such as the pupil whose book worried you, or the one who hasn't spoken since Tuesday.

Tracking participation is what keeps the deliberateness honest, because memory flatters everyone. A simple record shows you who has actually stayed invisible across lessons, and it's rarely who you'd have guessed.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

You keep a simple tally of who has answered across the week. Before Friday's lesson you read it back, find three pupils with no entries at all, and plan a question for each of them, matched to material they've had time to think about.

Try it

A way to begin

For one class, track who answers for a week, by whatever means is least effort. Read the record back before planning the next lesson, and let it choose two of your questions for you.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Who the record says you haven't heard from. It's worth comparing with who you thought you hadn't heard from, because the two lists are often different.
  • How the responses change once the class knows anyone might be asked, not just the raised hands.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • 'Where appropriate' is doing real work in the card. Which pupils to press, and when a quieter route to a pupil's thinking serves better than a public question, is your call, because you know the class, and no guide can make it for you.

Technology second

Where technology fits

An approved participation tracking tool in your setting can carry the record for you, and its contribution is memory rather than judgement. It shows you who has stayed invisible across lessons, but deciding who to call, and why, stays with you.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Hearing from all learners rather than a self-selecting few is central to Wiliam and Black's case for assessment that happens during the lesson, while it can still change the teaching. The card’s attribution stands as published: Wiliam, Black.
