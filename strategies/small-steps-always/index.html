Small Steps, Always

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 📚Small Steps, Always

Rosenshine: present new material in small steps with practice after each. Use animations to chunk information and reveal content gradually. Let students process before continuing.

Suggested technology slide animation; progressive reveal · Informed by Rosenshine

Find this under Explain difficult ideas · Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand

Part of Structuring Explanations in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

New material has a habit of arriving faster than pupils can process it, and Rosenshine's first principle is really clear about the remedy, which is to present new material in small steps with practice after each one, because working memory is narrow and it's easily overwhelmed.

The important thing is the practice after each step, because without the check a step is just a pause in the talking, and telling a class something isn't the same as explaining it to them.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Introducing balancing equations, you build the method up one move per slide, using animation to reveal each step as it's needed. After each reveal, pupils have a go at one line of a parallel problem on mini whiteboards before the next step comes up.

Try it

A way to begin

Take one explanation you've already got planned and mark where each genuinely new idea begins. Those marks are your steps, so add a short bit of practice after each one.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where things start to go wrong. With small steps, confusion shows up at a specific step rather than at the end of a long explanation, which makes it much easier to deal with.
  • Whether the practice after each step is being done by everyone, or just by the pupils who were already keeping up.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Step size is a judgement about this class and what they already know, and what's one step for a second teaching might be three steps for a first. You know the class, and no guide does.
  • Small steps aren't the same as slow teaching, because checking as you go can save you a much longer reteach later on.
