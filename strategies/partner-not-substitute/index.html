Partner, Not Substitute

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

24 🤖Partner, Not Substitute

Willingham: memory is the residue of thought. Offload the thinking to AI and nothing is learned. Have students use it to check and challenge their reasoning, not to do it for them.

Suggested technology AI as a critic of own work · Informed by Willingham

Find this under Use technology with greater purpose

Part of Digital Cognition in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Willingham's line, that memory is the residue of thought, is the whole argument here. A pupil who offloads the thinking to AI hasn't done any thinking, so there's no residue, and nothing gets learned, however polished the work looks.

The distinction the card draws is between AI producing the reasoning and AI challenging reasoning the pupil has already done. The first is substitution, and it hollows out the very capacity this strand is trying to build. The second can work like an external check, provided the judging of the critique stays with the pupil.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A pupil finishes a written argument, then asks the approved AI tool to find the weakest step in their reasoning. The tool points at a paragraph, and the pupil now has a decision to make, which is whether the critique is right and, if so, what changes. They revise, or they reject the critique with a reason, and either way the reasoning, both rounds of it, was theirs.

Try it

A way to begin

Trial it on work that's already finished, so the only thing the tool can touch is the checking. The order, thinking first and tool second, is the whole safeguard.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pupils who accept the critique wholesale. Handing your judgement over to the critic is offloading too, just one step later.
  • Whether the revisions show the pupil's own reasoning about the critique, or just compliance with it.
  • What the work looks like when the tool isn't available, because that's your check on whether any of the thinking has actually stuck.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • This sits inside your setting's policy on pupil AI use, and where pupils can't use such tools directly, the same critique step works with a peer or a mark scheme instead.
  • A pupil with nothing on the page yet has nothing for a critic to work on, so the card's sequence isn't optional.

Technology second

Where technology fits

Used within the card's framing, as a critic of work the pupil has already produced, an approved AI tool in your setting can supply the external challenge that self-monitoring needs. The moment it starts drafting, planning or fixing on the pupil's behalf, though, it's the tool doing the regulating, and a pupil who always has the answer handed to them ends up needing the tool. Scaffold the regulation, then fade the scaffold, because the goal here, as everywhere in this strand, is a learner who no longer needs the tool.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Willingham's memory-as-residue-of-thought principle does the heavy lifting here, because whatever the pupil doesn't think about, the pupil doesn't learn, whoever or whatever did the thinking instead. The card’s attribution stands as published: Willingham.
