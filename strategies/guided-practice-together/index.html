Guided Practice Together

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

17 👥Guided Practice Together

Independence is the destination, not the starting point. Before students work alone, have them practise with peers in structured steps, checking each other's responses at every stage before moving forward.

Suggested technology collaborative document tool · Informed by Rosenshine

Part of Students as Learning Resources in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Between your model and independent work there's a gap, and pupils tend to fall into it at really predictable points. Structured practice with peers, checked at every stage, catches them early, so nobody moves on to step four carrying an unnoticed error from step two.

The card's first line sets the frame: independence is the destination, not the starting point.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Learning to balance chemical equations, pairs work through four equations one at a time, comparing answers after each one and sorting out any disagreements before the next. You circulate listening out for pairs who agree on a wrong answer, because those are the errors that won't come to light on their own.

Try it

A way to begin

Take a practice set you already use and add one rule: check with your partner after every item, not at the end. That check after every item is what makes it guided practice rather than just marking each other's finished work.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pairs who agree quickly on wrong answers. Peer checking catches disagreement, but confident shared errors need you.
  • The point where the checking starts to slow down pupils who are ready to work alone, because that's your signal to start removing the structure rather than tightening it.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • It's the checking at every stage that makes this work. A check at the end of a whole exercise arrives after the errors have compounded, and a check at every stage stops them compounding at all.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Guided practice with a high success rate before independent work is one of Rosenshine's principles of instruction, drawn from studies of what the most effective teachers actually do. The card’s attribution stands as published: Rosenshine.
