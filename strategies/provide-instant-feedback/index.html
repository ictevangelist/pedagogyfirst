Provide Instant Feedback

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

21 ✅Provide Instant Feedback

Metcalfe shows instant feedback is essential for confident errors. For general retrieval, allow a processing gap before revealing answers. Speed matters most when confidence is highest.

Suggested technology quiz tool with instant answer reveal · Informed by Metcalfe

Part of Metacognition & Calibration in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

This card carries two instructions at once, and that's deliberate. Metcalfe shows that instant feedback is essential for confident errors, because the more certain a pupil was, the more the fast correction helps. For general retrieval though, it's worth leaving a little gap before revealing the answers, so the retrieval attempt has room to finish.

So the rule the card offers is really about knowing when to be quick. Speed matters most when the confidence is highest, and the rest of the time a pause does no harm at all.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Running a review quiz, you reveal each answer a beat or two after the question rather than instantly, which lets pupils commit to an answer. When a show of hands turns up a confidently wrong majority on one item, the delay disappears and you correct that misconception on the spot.

Try it

A way to begin

Next quiz, delay each reveal until every pupil has committed to an answer, and then break your own rule the moment you spot a confident error.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What pupils do in the gap before the reveal. If they're reconsidering and committing, the gap is being used well, and if they're just waiting, it isn't doing much for them.
  • Whether the corrected confident errors stay corrected the following week. Metcalfe's work suggests they often may, and it's worth seeing whether that holds for your class.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Instant feedback and instant answers are different things. Feedback follows an attempt, and a tool that supplies the answer at the first pause has replaced the attempt altogether.

Where this connects

  • 🔬 Teach the Illusion of Knowing Show students their own illusion of knowing. Ask them to rate confidence before revealing the answer. When they were certain and wrong, that is the precise moment to intervene. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • 🎯 Confidence Ratings Dunlosky and Metcalfe showed students are most confident about things most recently read, not things they actually know. Rate confidence after each attempt, then reveal the answer. That gap is the target. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • ❓ The Generation Effect Slamecka and Graf found generating an answer, even a wrong one, before being told it produces stronger retention than passive exposure. The effort of generation drives encoding, not the accuracy. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties
  • ⏰ Immediate for Simple Tasks For simple or procedural tasks, correct errors immediately. Shute found quick feedback prevents mistakes from embedding. Do not wait until next lesson to fix what can be fixed now. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency
  • 🕐 Delayed for Complex Tasks For complex work, a short delay before feedback lets the learner process first. Immediate correction on difficult tasks can short-circuit thinking. Let the struggle do its work. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency

Technology second

Where technology fits

Quiz tools with instant answer reveal are built for exactly half of this card. The reveal speed is usually a setting, so it's worth checking whether the approved tool in your setting can hold the answers back until the responses are in. And if the format starts to resemble a fastest finger first round, rewarding speed over thought, it's taking away the difficulty that makes the retrieval worth doing.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits
