Spaced Retrieval Practice

20 🔁Spaced Retrieval Practice

Low stakes quizzing on prior content, not today's lesson. Returning to older material at increasing intervals is one of the most robust findings in memory research. Re-reading notes is not the same as retrieving.

Suggested technology spaced repetition quiz tool · Informed by Bjork

Find this under Help pupils remember more · Help pupils connect prior and new learning

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Most quizzing looks at what was taught today or this week, which is really useful for checking the teaching worked, but it can't tell you whether pupils will still have that learning next month. That's where spaced retrieval comes in. As the card notes, returning to older material at increasing intervals is one of the most robust findings in memory research, and the idea at the heart of it is simple: coming back to something after you've had time to forget a little of it does far more for remembering than going over it again straight away.

The reason it sits with formative assessment is that the results tell you something you can use. If lots of the class have forgotten something, you know what needs revisiting, and you know in good time, before that knowledge is needed again.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Your weekly low stakes history quiz might have two questions from last week, two from the last unit, and one from all the way back in the autumn term. If the autumn question is the one most pupils get wrong, you put it back into the quiz in a different form the following week.

Try it

A way to begin

Have a go by adding one deliberately old question to a quiz routine you already run. Even one question will start to show you what's faded, and you can build up from there.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How pupils do on recent material compared with older material. It's completely normal for the older material to be weaker, and that difference is exactly what the spacing is there to help with.
  • How pupils react to getting the older questions wrong. For this to work they need to feel that a wrong answer is fine and just shows what needs another look, so it's worth keeping an eye on whether the low stakes genuinely feel low stakes to them.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • It can be tempting for pupils to prepare by re-reading their notes, and the card is explicit that re-reading notes is not the same as retrieving. Pupils will often default to re-reading because it feels easier, so it's worth being clear with them about the difference.
  • How far apart to space the returns is a professional judgement for the teacher in the room, made with this class and this content in front of them. The increasing intervals are the principle, but when the returns actually happen is up to you, because you know your class and your content better than any schedule written by someone else.

  • 📅 Distribute Practice Over Time Spread retrieval across days and weeks rather than cramming it into one session. Build review points into your medium-term plan. The calendar is part of the pedagogy. 01 Retrieval Practice · Spacing & Interleaving
  • 🧠 Retrieval Beats Re-reading Replace re-reading with retrieval. Instead of reviewing notes, ask students to write down everything they remember, then check. The testing is the learning, not the preparation for it. 01 Retrieval Practice · The Testing Effect

  • 🧪 Spaced Self Quizzing Teach students to quiz themselves rather than re-read notes. Re-reading feels productive but produces weak retention. Self quizzing feels harder but works. Retrieval effort drives long term memory. 02 Formative Assessment · Students as Owners of Their Learning

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

The thinking behind this comes from Bjork's work on desirable difficulties, which found that spacing and retrieval both make practice feel harder in the moment, but that this difficulty is a good sign, because it's doing the work that helps the learning stick for the longer term. The card’s attribution stands as published: Bjork.
