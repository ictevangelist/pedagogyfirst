Questions That Drive Enquiry

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

24 📄Questions That Drive Enquiry

King found connecting questions to prior knowledge produces the most powerful learning. Ask students to write what they know and want to know. Their questions become the entry point.

Suggested technology shared doc, padlet or survey tool · Informed by King

Find this under Help pupils connect prior and new learning

Part of Student-Generated Questions in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

The know and want-to-know routine is familiar enough to feel like habit, but King's research gives it a firmer footing, because questions connected to prior knowledge produce the most powerful learning, and asking students to write what they know and what they want to know brings both halves out at once.

Their questions become the way into the content, rather than an opening activity that gets filed away and forgotten.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Opening a unit on space, pupils write two short lists, what they already know and what they want to know. You read both before planning the next lesson. The first list shows the prior knowledge to connect to, along with one misconception to unpick, and the second supplies three questions that are good enough to teach through.

Try it

A way to begin

Give ten minutes at the start of your next new topic to know and want to know, written down and collected in rather than just discussed.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Prior knowledge you didn't expect, in both directions.
  • Which pupil questions could anchor a whole lesson, and which ones reveal what the class thinks the topic is about.
  • Whether pupils spot their own questions being answered as the unit unfolds.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • It's a starting point rather than the whole route. The scheme of work still sets the destination, and what to do with the questions it can't accommodate is your call.
  • Collecting the lists in writing keeps every pupil's starting point visible, and not just the confident talkers'.
