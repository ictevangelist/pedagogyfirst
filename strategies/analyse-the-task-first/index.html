Analyse the Task First

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

03 🔍Analyse the Task First

Zimmerman: strong learners break a task into its demands before starting. Ask students what this question is really asking and which prior topics it draws on before they answer.

Suggested technology annotate the question on a board · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Develop metacognition · Help pupils connect prior and new learning

Part of Forethought & Planning in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

The first minute of a task is often spent writing, and that's actually the problem. Zimmerman found that strong learners break a task down into its demands before they start, whereas weaker learners answer the question they assumed they were asked, and they only find out at marking time.

Two questions do most of the work here. What is this question really asking, and which topics that you've already covered does it draw on? Both of these come before the first line goes down.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

A worded maths problem goes up on the board. Before anyone solves it, you annotate it together as a class, so the quantity that's actually being asked for gets circled, the topics it draws on get named at the side, and the step most likely to catch people out gets a warning mark. Only then do pupils have a go at it, and the annotation stays up where they can see it.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pupils who start writing the moment the task lands. Starting quickly isn't really a good sign here.
  • Whether the demands pupils identify match the ones you had in mind, because the mismatches tell you what to model next.
  • Misreadings coming to light during the analysis rather than in your marking pile, which is the strategy doing exactly what it's there for.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Model the analysis out loud before you expect pupils to do it, because the questions look obvious to an expert precisely because the expert no longer notices themselves asking them.
  • It does cost a few minutes at the start of the task, so budget for them, but you'll usually find they get repaid at the end.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Task analysis is the opening move of Zimmerman's forethought phase, and it's the reading of the demands that everything downstream, the goal, the strategy and the monitoring, depends on. The card’s attribution stands as published: Zimmerman.
