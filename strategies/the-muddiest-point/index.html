The Muddiest Point

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

22 🌧️The Muddiest Point

At lesson close, each student anonymously writes the one thing they are least clear about. The teacher addresses the most common muddy points publicly at the start of the next lesson. Two minutes; high value.

Suggested technology anonymous response tool · Informed by Angelo & Cross

Find this under Check understanding · Inclusive practice: access and participation · Try this tomorrow

Part of Students as Owners of Their Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Asking a class whether they understand invites just about the least reliable answer in teaching. Asking each pupil, anonymously, what they're least clear about gets you the truth, because nobody has to own a confusion in public.

Two minutes at the end of a lesson gets you the agenda for the start of the next one, drawn from the pupils' actual sticking points rather than your best guess at them.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

In the last two minutes, every pupil writes the one thing from today they're least sure about, with no names, and the slips go in a box on the way out. Three pupils name the same step in the method, so the next lesson opens by reteaching that step to everyone.

Try it

A way to begin

Run it tomorrow with slips of paper or an anonymous form, whichever is less fuss. The only rule that really matters is that the most common muddy point gets addressed, publicly, next lesson.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where the answers cluster. One pupil's confusion is individual, but eight naming the same idea is a teaching point for everyone.
  • The pupils who write that nothing is muddy, every single time. That much certainty, that consistently, is worth a careful check of its own.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The answers have to visibly change what happens next. If muddy points vanish into the box and never come back out, pupils stop telling you the truth within a fortnight.
  • Keep it anonymous, because the honesty is what you're collecting, and it disappears the moment names go on slips.

Technology second

Where technology fits

An anonymous response tool on your setting's approved list makes the collection easy and genuinely nameless, which protects the honesty this all depends on. Slips of paper in a box do the same job, so choose whichever your pupils will trust.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

The technique comes out of the classroom assessment tradition in higher education, and it survives in schools for the same reason, which is honest evidence at almost no cost. The card’s attribution stands as published: Angelo & Cross.
