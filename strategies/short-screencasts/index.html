Short Screencasts

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

17 🎥Short Screencasts

Record short screencasts with narration over visuals. Mayer: images plus voice outperforms text-heavy slides. Keep each video to one idea. Brevity beats completeness every time.

Suggested technology screen recording tool; Loom or similar · Informed by Mayer

Part of Multimedia & Dual Coding in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

A long recorded explanation is easy to make, but pupils rarely get much out of one. Mayer's evidence points at images plus voice outperforming text-heavy slides, and the card adds the discipline that goes with it, which is one idea per video, because brevity beats completeness.

You don't need any single clip to cover everything, because that's what the growing library is for, so each clip only has to do one job.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

You record one screencast on expanding a single bracket, with the working shown on screen and your voice narrating over it, and nothing else. The next idea, a second bracket, gets its own separate recording.

Try it

A way to begin

Record one short screencast this week on the single idea you're asked to repeat most often.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which clips get replayed, and where pupils pause them along the way.
  • Whether the repeat explanations that prompted the recording in the first place actually reduce.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • One idea per video is the whole discipline, so when a recording starts needing chapters, it's really several videos.
  • These are supplements to your live explanation rather than a substitute for it, and the strand is firm on that distinction.
  • How short is short is your call to make, and the reason to keep making it is working memory. It's narrow, and every extra idea in a video competes for the space the first one needs, so when in doubt, split the recording.

Where this connects

  • 🎧 Narrate, Don’t Label Mayer’s modality: people learn better from images with narration than images with text. Record a narrated screencast over a diagram. Let the voice do what text would overload. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
  • 📝 Worked Examples on Replay Sweller and Cooper: students learn more from worked examples than equivalent problems. Record a screencast of the example so students can pause and study the method at their pace. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Modelling & Worked Examples
  • ➕ Supplement, Never Substitute The EEF: technology works best when it supplements teaching, not replaces it. A screencast works alongside classroom explanation, not instead of it. Supplement, never substitute. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Structuring Explanations
  • ✂️ Chunk and Reveal Mayer’s segmenting: break content into chunks. Use slide animations to reveal one idea per click, not auto-play. Let working memory process before the next chunk arrives. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load

Technology second

Where technology fits

The approved screen recording tool in your setting is all this needs, so record the screen, narrate over it and share it through your usual platform. Keep the design rules from the rest of the strand inside the recording too, with minimal text, one clear visual and the voice doing the explaining.
