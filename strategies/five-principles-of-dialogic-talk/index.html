14 💬Five Principles of Dialogic Talk

Alexander identifies five qualities of dialogic talk: collective, reciprocal, supportive, cumulative and purposeful. Most talk meets two or three of these. Audit and start with the gaps.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Alexander names five qualities of dialogic talk: collective, reciprocal, supportive, cumulative and purposeful. The list works as a diagnostic, because most classroom talk already meets two or three of the five.

That is what makes this card practical rather than aspirational. You are not building dialogic talk from nothing; you are finding the missing qualities and working on those. Audit, and start with the gaps.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A teacher asks a colleague to observe a discussion lesson with just the five words on a slip of paper. The verdict: collective and supportive, clearly; reciprocal, sometimes; cumulative, rarely. Contributions sat side by side without building on each other. That one word, cumulative, becomes the focus for the term.

Try it

A way to begin

Audit one discussion this week against the five qualities, honestly. Two or three ticks is the expected result, not a failure.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which of the five are reliably present in your classroom talk, and which never appear.
  • Whether contributions chain, each building on the last, or simply accumulate.
  • Whether the talk is going somewhere planned, which is what purposeful asks.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Start with the gaps rather than everything at once; the card is explicit on this.
  • Several other cards in this cluster target one quality each: ground rules build the collective and supportive, building on responses builds the cumulative. The audit tells you which to reach for.

Where this connects

  • ▶️ Break the IRE Pattern IRE (Initiation, Response, Evaluation): ask, answer, judge. Alexander shows this pattern limits thinking. Replace evaluation with a follow-up question. What makes you say that? 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 🗣 Exploratory Talk Mercer distinguishes disputational, cumulative and exploratory talk. Only exploratory talk, where students challenge ideas with evidence and build on each thinking, improves outcomes. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 📚 Ground Rules for Talk Mercer found teaching norms of exploratory talk transforms discussion. Norms co-constructed with the class outperform those displayed or modelled. Make the ground rules together. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 🔁 Build on Student Responses Alexander’s EEF programme showed gains when teachers extended rather than evaluated. Return an answer: Aisha says X. Does anyone want to add or challenge? Conductor, not judge. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
