Delayed for Complex Tasks

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

07 🕐Delayed for Complex Tasks

For complex work, a short delay before feedback lets the learner process first. Immediate correction on difficult tasks can short-circuit thinking. Let the struggle do its work.

Suggested technology LMS with scheduled feedback release · Informed by Shute; Kulhavy

Part of Timing & Frequency in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Being quick with feedback isn't always the right call. On complex work, the research Shute and Kulhavy report points to a short delay before feedback, because immediate correction can cut off the very thinking the task was set to provoke.

A pupil midway through a genuinely hard problem doesn't need the answer arriving. They need the time to work at it, and feedback that lands too early can turn a thinking task into a copying one.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

After pupils attempt a multi-step physics problem, you collect the attempts in and say nothing that lesson. The feedback comes at the start of the next one, once pupils have a bit of distance from their own reasoning, and it begins by asking them to name their sticking point before they read the comment.

Try it

A way to begin

Choose one genuinely complex task and deliberately hold your feedback back for a day. It's worth noticing what you would have interrupted if you'd stepped in straight away.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils persist for longer when they know correction isn't coming immediately.
  • What pupils ask you during the task when they can't hand the struggle straight back to you, because the questions change, and usually for the better.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • This card and Immediate for Simple Tasks are really one principle rather than two, which is to match the timing to the complexity of the task. Which task counts as which is a judgement for the teacher who knows the class and the content.
  • Delay means a short delay here. A day or a lesson lets the processing happen, and two weeks is just slow marking.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • ⏰ Immediate for Simple Tasks For simple or procedural tasks, correct errors immediately. Shute found quick feedback prevents mistakes from embedding. Do not wait until next lesson to fix what can be fixed now. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency
  • 🧔 Embrace the Struggle Bjork coined desirable difficulties for conditions that slow apparent learning but improve long-term retention. Effortful retrieval is the clearest example. Resist the urge to make it easier. 01 Retrieval Practice · Desirable Difficulties
  • 💡 Feedback Must Cause Thinking Write "why might this be stronger with evidence?" not insert the evidence yourself. If feedback needs no effort from the learner, it is information, not feedback. Thinking is the point. 03 Feedback · Feedback & the Learner
  • 🔄 During, Not Just After Feedback given while students are still working is formative. Feedback returned weeks later is a post-mortem. Move around the room, intervene in the moment. Proximity is strategy. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Shute's review and Kulhavy's earlier work both found that feedback timing interacts with task difficulty, so there's no single right speed, only a right speed for this task. The card’s attribution stands as published: Shute; Kulhavy.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Feedback.
