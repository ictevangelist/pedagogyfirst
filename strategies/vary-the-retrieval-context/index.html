Vary the Retrieval Context

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

13 🔀Vary the Retrieval Context

Retrieve the same content in different formats and settings, decontextualising knowledge so it is not tied to one context or format. Harder for students; that is precisely the point.

Suggested technology varied formats; multi-modal tools · Informed by Godden & Baddeley; Bjork

Find this under Help pupils connect prior and new learning

Part of Desirable Difficulties in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

There are lots of ways knowledge can get stuck in the format it was practised in. A pupil who can answer a question in a quiz can really struggle to recognise the same question when it turns up in an essay, and it's not that the knowledge isn't there, it's that it's tied to the situation they learned it in.

Godden and Baddeley showed decades ago that recall is bound to context. The card's response is to retrieve the same content in different formats and settings, so the knowledge gradually comes loose from any one of them. It does make things harder for students, but this is one of those places where the harder version is the one doing them some good.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

One week your class retrieves the digestive system by labelling a diagram, the next by writing a paragraph from memory, and later on by answering spoken questions with no image in sight. The content stays exactly the same each time, and it's just the format around it that keeps moving.

Try it

A way to begin

Take some content the class has only ever retrieved one way and switch the format once, such as diagram to prose, written to spoken, or multiple choice to free recall. See what travels and what doesn't.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How pupils do when the format changes. Some drop in performance is completely normal, and it shows you how much of the knowledge was tied to the old format.
  • Whose knowledge already travels across formats and whose is bound to just one. For that second group, it's worth remembering they may have learned the format as much as the content.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Vary the retrieval rather than the goal. What's being retrieved stays the same, and it's only the packaging around it that moves, because otherwise you're testing something new rather than strengthening something taught.

Where this connects

  • 🎲 Interleave Topics, Don't Block Kornell and Bjork found mixing topics produces stronger retention than finishing one before moving to the next. Blocked practice feels organised; it produces weaker retention. Discriminating is the mechanism. 01 Retrieval Practice · Spacing & Interleaving

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Godden and Baddeley's diving study is the classic demonstration that recall is tied to the context it was learned in, and Bjork's work on varied practice shows how teachers can put that finding to use. The card’s attribution stands as published: Godden & Baddeley; Bjork.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Retrieval Practice.
