Fade the Scaffold

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

18 🎓Fade the Scaffold

Zimmerman: support must be withdrawn for self-regulation to develop. Move from modelled to guided to independent use, removing prompts as students show they no longer need them.

Suggested technology prompts gradually removed · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Build independence · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Strategy Knowledge & Use in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

For self-regulation to develop, the support does have to come away at some point, and Zimmerman is really clear on this. If a pupil is always prompted to plan, check and reflect, they never get the opportunity to remember to do it for themselves, and that's the bit we're actually after.

The route runs from modelled to guided to independent use, with prompts removed as pupils show they no longer need them. Self-regulation has to be taught, modelled, practised and gradually handed over, and it's that gradual handing over this card focuses on.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Your essay planning prompts follow a planned retreat across the year, so there's a full prompt sheet modelled and used in the autumn, a shortened cue card in the spring, and by the summer a blank page, with the cue card still available to the pupils whose work shows they need it.

Try it

A way to begin

Choose one prompt that's been in place so long it never gets questioned, and decide what evidence would tell you it can start to fade.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What happens on the first unprompted task, because that's the honest measure of whether the scaffold was teaching or just substituting.
  • Pupils still receiving prompts they clearly no longer need. Fading late has costs too, because support pupils have outgrown just gets in the way of the independence you're after.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Fade by readiness rather than by the calendar, and not necessarily whole-class either. Who's ready is a judgement only you can make, in the room.
  • Fading isn't final either. A pupil who struggles without the prompt gets it back, and the fade simply restarts from there.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits
