Fade the Scaffold

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

18 🎓Fade the Scaffold

Zimmerman: support must be withdrawn for self-regulation to develop. Move from modelled to guided to independent use, removing prompts as students show they no longer need them.

Suggested technology prompts gradually removed · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Build independence · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Strategy Knowledge & Use in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

A scaffold that never comes down has become part of the building. Zimmerman's point is blunt: support must be withdrawn for self-regulation to develop, because a pupil who is always prompted to plan, check and reflect never has to remember to.

The route runs from modelled to guided to independent use, with prompts removed as pupils show they no longer need them. Self-regulation has to be taught, modelled, practised and gradually handed over; this card is the handing over.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Essay planning prompts follow a planned retreat across the year: a full prompt sheet modelled and used in the autumn, a shortened cue card in the spring, and by summer a blank page, with the cue card still available to the pupils whose work shows they need it.

Try it

A way to begin

Choose one prompt that has become permanent furniture in your classroom, and decide what evidence would tell you it can start to fade.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What happens on the first unprompted task. That is the honest measure of whether the scaffold was teaching or substituting.
  • Pupils still receiving prompts they demonstrably no longer need. Fading late has costs too; the support becomes a ceiling.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Fade by readiness, not by calendar, and not necessarily whole-class. Who is ready is a judgement only the teacher in the room can make.
  • Fading is not final. A pupil who struggles without the prompt gets it back, and the fade restarts from there.
