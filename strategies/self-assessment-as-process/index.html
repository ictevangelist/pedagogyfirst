Self-Assessment as Process

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

12 🔬Self-Assessment as Process

Ask students to assess their work against criteria before you mark it. Andrade found the act of comparing, not the accuracy of the judgment, improves performance. Process is the point.

Suggested technology self-review checklist tool · Informed by Andrade

Find this under Build independence · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Source & Direction in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Andrade's finding rearranges the usual worry about self-assessment. Pupils' judgements don't need to be accurate for the practice to work, because it's the act of comparing work against criteria that improves performance, so the process really is the point.

That takes a lot of the pressure off. You're not training examiners, you're giving pupils repeated practice at holding their work up against a standard, which is exactly the comparison Sadler's conditions require.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Before handing in an essay, pupils complete a short self-review against the criteria, saying where each one is met, where it isn't, and one change they've made as a result. The teacher marks the essay afterwards as usual, but reads the self-review first.

Try it

A way to begin

Add one line to your submission routine, which is to ask pupils to mark the place where they best meet the criteria and the place where they least do, before the work comes to you.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils' self-judgements move closer to yours over a term. Accuracy isn't the goal, but drift towards it suggests the comparing is genuine.
  • What pupils change between the self-review and submission, because a self-assessment that changes nothing was really just a form-filling exercise.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Self-assessment against criteria pupils don't understand is guesswork, so Sadler's first condition, knowing what quality looks like, comes before this card rather than after it.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Andrade's research sits within the same self-regulation tradition as Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick and Boud, which is feedback practice that builds the pupil's own evaluative judgement rather than just improving the next piece of work. The card’s attribution stands as published: Andrade.
