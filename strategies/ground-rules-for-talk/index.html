17 📚Ground Rules for Talk

Mercer found teaching norms of exploratory talk transforms discussion. Norms co-constructed with the class outperform those displayed or modelled. Make the ground rules together.

Suggested technology shared doc or display tool · Informed by Mercer

Find this under Improve classroom discussion

Part of Dialogic Teaching & Discussion in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Groups don't fall into exploratory talk by accident. Mercer found that teaching its norms transforms discussion, and there's one detail that changes how you should go about it, which is that norms co-constructed with the class outperform norms that are just displayed or modelled.

Writing the rules together is part of what makes them work, because rules a class wrote for themselves are rules they can hold each other to.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Your class spends ten minutes drafting its own rules for discussion, such as everyone's ideas get considered, reasons are asked for, and we try to agree before we move on. The wording is the pupils' own, and that's what gives the poster on the wall any force it has.

Try it

A way to begin

Before the next discussion-heavy topic, give ten minutes to agreeing the ground rules with the class instead of presenting yours to them.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils bring up the rules unprompted during group work.
  • Who enforces them when they get broken, you or the group, because the second one is the sign they've really taken hold.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • It really does need to be co-constructed. The card is explicit that displayed or modelled norms underperform, so it's worth resisting the efficient shortcut even when time is tight.
  • Success criteria built with the class work in a similar way, which is why the formative assessment strand has a sibling card.

Where this connects

  • 🗣 Exploratory Talk Mercer distinguishes disputational, cumulative and exploratory talk. Only exploratory talk, where students challenge ideas with evidence and build on each thinking, improves outcomes. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 👨‍🏫 Accountable Talk Michaels, O’Connor and Resnick hold students to accurate knowledge and rigorous reasoning. Teach moves: I agree because..., The evidence is..., Say more.... Language shapes thinking. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 🤝 Build Success Criteria Together Ask students what excellent looks like before they begin. Their language, not yours, becomes the standard. Criteria they articulate themselves land far more deeply than criteria handed to them. 02 Formative Assessment · Clarify Learning Intentions
  • 💬 Five Principles of Dialogic Talk Alexander identifies five qualities of dialogic talk: collective, reciprocal, supportive, cumulative and purposeful. Most talk meets two or three of these. Audit and start with the gaps. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
