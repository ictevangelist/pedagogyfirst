Retrieval Beats Re-reading

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 🧠Retrieval Beats Re-reading

Replace re-reading with retrieval. Instead of reviewing notes, ask students to write down everything they remember, then check. The testing is the learning, not the preparation for it.

Suggested technology low-stakes quiz or flashcard tool · Informed by Roediger & Karpicke

Find this under Help pupils remember more

Part of The Testing Effect in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Ask a class how they revise and most of them will describe some version of reading their notes again. It's completely understandable, because re-reading feels productive while you're doing it. The trouble is that the feeling is misleading. Reading something again mostly gives pupils a sense of familiarity with the page, and familiarity isn't the same thing as being able to remember the material when they need it.

Roediger and Karpicke showed this really clearly. Students who were tested on material remembered far more of it weeks later than students who spent the same amount of time re-reading it. The card puts the implication plainly: the testing is the learning, not the preparation for it.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Before an end-of-topic check, an English class spends ten minutes writing down what they can remember about a poem's imagery, with their anthology notes closed. Then they open the notes and check what they've written against them. It takes the same amount of time as a read-through would have done, but the effort has gone into remembering rather than reading, and the research says that's the effort that makes the difference.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How pupils react when the notes close. If they're reluctant to have a go without them, that often tells you they've mistaken familiarity for knowing.
  • What the checking stage turns up. The gaps pupils find for themselves here are the ones that re-reading would have hidden from them, so they're worth paying attention to.
  • Whether confidence and recall actually match up. Pupils who felt they knew the material from reading it can be surprised by how little of it they can produce when asked.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • This works best as a straight swap for re-reading rather than something extra on top. The time pupils would have spent going over their notes becomes the retrieval attempt plus the check, so it doesn't add anything to anyone's workload.
  • You can expect a bit of resistance at first, because retrieving feels harder than reading. That difficulty is normal, and the research suggests it's actually part of what makes the practice work, so it isn't a sign that something has gone wrong.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

This is the testing effect, and it's one of the best evidenced findings in the guide. Roediger and Karpicke found that retrieval did more for retention weeks later than re-studying did, and Dunlosky's reviews rank practice testing among the most effective study techniques available. The card’s attribution stands as published: Roediger & Karpicke.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Retrieval Practice.
