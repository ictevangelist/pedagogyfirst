Build In Time to Act

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

14 ⏰Build In Time to Act

Feedback with no time to act on it has no value. Begin the next lesson with students reading and responding to feedback before anything new. Time to act is where the learning happens.

Suggested technology LMS; share feedback in advance · Informed by Wiliam & Leahy

Find this under Help pupils act on feedback · Inclusive practice: feedback and next steps

Part of Feedback That Moves Learning Forward in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Feedback given at the end of a unit, with nothing left to apply it to, can't change anything. The card's fix is a structural one, so the next lesson begins with pupils reading and responding to their feedback before anything new arrives.

It's worth being honest about what's at stake here, because those first few minutes are what make the marking you've already done worth the hours it took.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Books go back at the start of the lesson, and pupils spend the opening ten minutes on their responses, answering the questions left in the margins, redrafting one sentence, or correcting a method. New content waits until the responses are done.

Try it

A way to begin

Protect the first ten minutes of one lesson this week for acting on feedback. Nothing else changes, and the feedback you were going to give anyway now has time set aside for pupils to actually use it.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What pupils do with the time without being prompted, which tells you how actionable the feedback actually was.
  • Whether the responses engage with the gap you identified. A tick and 'done' next to an unchanged answer tells you the response hasn't really happened.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Sharing feedback in advance, where your school's systems allow it, means the lesson time starts at the responding rather than the reading.
  • If there's never time to act on it, the honest question is whether the feedback needed writing at all. The card puts it more bluntly: feedback with no time to act on it has no value.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Wiliam and Leahy treat time to act as part of the feedback itself rather than an optional follow-up, because feedback moves learning forward only while the work can still change. The card’s attribution stands as published: Wiliam & Leahy.
