Narrate, Don’t Label

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

14 🎧Narrate, Don’t Label

Mayer’s modality: people learn better from images with narration than images with text. Record a narrated screencast over a diagram. Let the voice do what text would overload.

Suggested technology screen recording with voiceover · Informed by Mayer

Find this under Inclusive practice: language and explanation

Part of Multimedia & Dual Coding in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

An image explained by on-screen text asks the eyes to do two jobs at once. Mayer's modality principle found that people learn better from images with narration than from images with text, because the voice takes one channel and the image takes the other.

Let the voice do what text would overload, as the card has it. The strand prose makes the same call, which is to narrate a diagram rather than captioning it.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Recording an explanation of longshore drift, a teacher screencasts the diagram and speaks the explanation over it, with the cursor tracing the movement being described. There are no paragraphs on the screen, so the words arrive by ear while the eyes follow the coast.

Try it

A way to begin

Take one diagram you usually present with a written explanation beside it, strip the text back to bare labels, and speak the explanation instead, either live or recorded.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where pupils look while you're narrating, compared with when there was text to read.
  • On a recorded version, where pupils pause and replay, because those points can map the difficult moments.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Modality moves the explanatory prose to the voice, but short labels that simply name the parts still belong on the image, and that's what the contiguity card covers.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🔇 Say It or Show It, Not Both Sweller’s redundancy effect: narrating text on screen forces double processing. Say it or show it, not both. Use the B or W key to blank the slide while you talk through the idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 🎥 Short Screencasts Record short screencasts with narration over visuals. Mayer: images plus voice outperforms text-heavy slides. Keep each video to one idea. Brevity beats completeness every time. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
  • 📍 Keep Words Near Images Mayer’s spatial contiguity: place words near the images they describe. Labels sit beside the diagram, not in a separate list. Use slide design to keep related information together. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
  • 🖼️ Words and Images Together Paivio’s dual coding: the brain processes words and images through separate channels. Design every slide with both: a key visual paired with minimal text. Two routes to the same idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
