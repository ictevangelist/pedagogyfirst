Connect to Prior Knowledge

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

03 🔗Connect to Prior Knowledge

Rosenshine: connect to prior knowledge before introducing anything new. Use a retrieval starter or concept map on screen to activate what students know. Link before you launch.

Suggested technology concept map or retrieval tool · Informed by Rosenshine

Find this under Explain difficult ideas · Help pupils connect prior and new learning

Part of Structuring Explanations in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Nothing you explain lands on a blank slate, and Rosenshine's advice is to connect to prior knowledge before you introduce anything new, because the new idea has to attach to something that's already there.

Link before you launch, as the card puts it. The few minutes you spend activating what pupils already know might well decide how much of the explanation actually sticks.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Before starting a unit on the causes of the First World War, a history teacher puts a concept map on the screen and asks the class to retrieve what they can remember about empire and alliances from the previous term. The gaps that show up then shape how the new explanation begins.

Try it

A way to begin

Open your next new topic with a two-minute retrieval starter on the specific ideas the explanation depends on, rather than general revision.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What the starter shows you. If the prior knowledge isn't there, the explanation you'd planned needs adjusting before it starts.
  • Whether pupils use the retrieved ideas of their own accord when they meet the new material.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The starter is there to tell you something, not to fill the first five minutes, so be ready to act on what it shows, even if that means reteaching before you launch into the new content.
  • Choose retrieval targets that genuinely underpin the new content, because activating unrelated knowledge just fills working memory without helping the explanation along.
