Think, Pair, Share: Disciplined

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

11 💬Think, Pair, Share: Disciplined

Individual thinking first; paired discussion second; nominated response third. Nomination makes the report-back evidence, not just the most confident voice. Without it, the share is optional.

Suggested technology timer for paired discussion · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Increase participation · Inclusive practice: participation and response

Part of Elicit Evidence of Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Think, pair, share tends to fail in really predictable ways. There's no individual thinking before the talking starts, there are pairs where one voice does all the work, and there's a share stage answered by whoever volunteers. The disciplined version closes each of those gaps in turn.

The nomination is the part doing the most work. When any pupil might report back, the pair talk has to leave both partners able to answer, and what gets shared tells you about the room rather than just about the most confident voice in it.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

In RE, pupils get one silent minute to note their own answer, two timed minutes to compare in pairs, and then you name who reports back. Because nobody knew who would be chosen, both partners in each pair had a reason to make sure both of them could answer.

Try it

A way to begin

Add the timer and the nomination to a think, pair, share you already use, and tell the class before the talk begins that you'll be choosing who shares, because it's that heads-up that gets everyone thinking rather than waiting.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether the silent minute is actually silent. The individual think is the stage most often skipped, and it's the one the paired talk depends on.
  • What nominated pupils report back, whether it's their own view, their partner's, or a genuine combination of the two.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • A visible timer for the pair stage holds the structure in place without you having to police it.
  • Nominating kindly is a skill in itself, and how much warning a particular pupil needs before speaking to the room is your judgement, because you know them.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 👥 Think-Pair-Share Lyman’s think-pair-share gives every student thinking time before public exposure. Pairs test ideas with a peer first. The result is more considered and more substantive discussion. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • 📝 Write Before You Speak Ask students to write before any verbal response is taken. Writing prevents the quickest from foreclosing others’ thinking. The pen democratises the room before the voice takes over. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • ⏰ Wait Time 1: After You Ask Rowe found most teachers wait under one second before speaking. Extending to three seconds increases the length and complexity of responses. Silence is thinking, not a gap to fill. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
  • 🎙️ Deliberate Cold Call Select who answers deliberately, not at random. Track participation so nobody stays invisible across lessons. Where appropriate, target the students whose thinking you most need to hear. 02 Formative Assessment · Elicit Evidence of Learning
