Remove the Clutter

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

10 ❌Remove the Clutter

Mayer’s coherence: remove every word, image and sound that does not support learning. Strip clip art from slides. Cut background music. Every element on screen must earn its place.

Suggested technology clean slide template; minimal design · Informed by Mayer

Find this under Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand

Part of Managing Cognitive Load in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Busy slides feel rich, but most of what makes them busy isn't helping anyone learn. Mayer's coherence principle is really firm on this, so remove every word, image and sound that doesn't support the learning, because each one competes for attention just when the explanation needs the space.

Clip art, background music and a themed border aren't neutral either, because if something is on the screen, pupils are processing it, whether you wanted them to or not.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Rebuilding a slide sequence on the digestive system, you delete the cartoon stomach, the sound effects and two of the three photographs, leaving one clear diagram and a handful of words. The content being taught is exactly the same, and there's simply less for it to compete with.

Try it

A way to begin

Open tomorrow's deck and remove everything decorative from a single slide. Teach it, and see whether anything is actually missed.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where attention settles once the decoration has gone.
  • Whether recall centres on the idea itself or on whatever came along with it.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The cuts that feel hardest to make tend to tell you the most. The test isn't whether an element is engaging but whether it supports the learning, and that's where the coherence principle draws the line.
  • Decoration for one class might be scaffold for another, so what supports learning is judged in the room rather than in the template.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🧠 Four Slots, No More Sweller: working memory holds about four items. Every screen element competes for those slots. Strip slides to essentials. Press B to blank the screen when you want attention on you. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 🔇 Say It or Show It, Not Both Sweller’s redundancy effect: narrating text on screen forces double processing. Say it or show it, not both. Use the B or W key to blank the slide while you talk through the idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 💡 Clarity Over Novelty The EEF found clarity matters more than delivery method. A diagram under a visualiser can outperform a polished video. Choose the tool that makes the idea clearest, not the newest. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Structuring Explanations
  • ➡️ Signal What Matters Mayer’s signalling: bold, arrows and highlights direct attention to what matters. Use annotation tools to signal live during the explanation. Without it, learners search alone. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
