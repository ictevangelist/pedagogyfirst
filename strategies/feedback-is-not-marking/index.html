03 Feedback · What Feedback Is

Feedback Is Not Marking

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 🔍Feedback Is Not Marking

If a student never reads or acts on your comments, you have marked but not given feedback. Before returning work, plan how students will engage with it. No engagement, no feedback.

Suggested technology audio or digital ink feedback tool · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Make feedback more useful

Part of What Feedback Is in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Hours of marking can produce no feedback at all. If a pupil never reads or acts on your comments, you have marked the work but nothing has been fed back. No engagement, no feedback.

The distinction changes where the effort goes. Marking is finished when the pen goes down; feedback is finished when the pupil has done something because of it. So the engagement has to be planned before the work goes back, not hoped for afterwards.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A history teacher returning essays plans the first ten minutes of the next lesson before writing a single comment: pupils will reread their feedback, rewrite one sentence in response, and be ready to say what their comment asked of them. The comments then get written knowing exactly how they will be used.

Try it

A way to begin

Before you mark your next set, decide what pupils will do with the comments and when. If you cannot find that time, mark less and protect it.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils read comments unprompted or wait to be told to.
  • How many of your comments lead to a visible change in the work.
  • How long the engagement takes compared with how long the marking took.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Engagement needs lesson time, which means something else gives way. That trade-off is a planning decision, not an afterthought.
  • A comment written for a pupil to act on reads differently from a comment written to show the work was marked. It helps to know which one you are writing.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • ⏱️ Build In Time to Act Feedback with no time to act on it is wasted. Begin the next lesson with students reading and responding to comments before anything new. The response is the learning event. 03 Feedback · Closing the Gap
  • 💡 Feedback Must Cause Thinking Write "why might this be stronger with evidence?" not insert the evidence yourself. If feedback needs no effort from the learner, it is information, not feedback. Thinking is the point. 03 Feedback · Feedback & the Learner
  • 📊 Reduce Volume, Raise Focus A page of comments overwhelms. One or two precise targets land. Identify the one thing that would make the biggest difference right now and focus entirely on that. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency
  • ✏️ Comment Only Marking Remove grades from work in progress. Research showed comments alone produced the greatest gains. Adding a score to a comment cancels it out: students read the grade and ignore the feedback entirely. 02 Formative Assessment · Feedback That Moves Learning Forward

Technology second

Where technology fits

Audio comments and digital ink can shorten the gap between work and response to almost nothing, and feedback that arrives while the work is still fresh has a far better chance of shaping what pupils do next. The tool does not change the rule, though: a recorded comment nobody listens to is still marking, not feedback. If you use the approved audio or annotation tool in your setting, plan the listening and the response with the same care as the recording.
