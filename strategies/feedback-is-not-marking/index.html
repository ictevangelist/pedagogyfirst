Feedback Is Not Marking

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 🔍Feedback Is Not Marking

If a student never reads or acts on your comments, you have marked but not given feedback. Before returning work, plan how students will engage with it. No engagement, no feedback.

Suggested technology audio or digital ink feedback tool · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Make feedback more useful

Part of What Feedback Is in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

You can spend hours marking and produce no feedback at all, because if a pupil never reads or acts on your comments, you've marked the work but nothing has actually been fed back. Without that engagement, there's simply no feedback.

The distinction really changes where the effort goes. Marking is finished when you put the pen down, but feedback isn't finished until the pupil has done something because of it, so the engagement has to be planned before the work goes back rather than hoped for afterwards.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Returning essays, you plan the first ten minutes of the next lesson before writing a single comment. Pupils will reread their feedback, rewrite one sentence in response, and be ready to say what their comment asked of them, which means your comments get written knowing exactly how they'll be used.

Try it

A way to begin

Before you mark your next set, decide what pupils will do with the comments and when they'll do it. If you can't find that time, it's worth marking less and protecting it.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils read comments without being prompted, or wait to be told to.
  • How many of your comments actually lead to a visible change in the work.
  • How long the engagement takes compared with how long the marking took, because the gap between the two can be really telling.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Engagement needs lesson time, which means something else has to give way, and that trade-off is a planning decision rather than an afterthought.
  • A comment written for a pupil to act on reads differently from a comment written to show the work was marked, and it really helps to know which one you're writing.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • ⏱️ Build In Time to Act Feedback with no time to act on it is wasted. Begin the next lesson with students reading and responding to comments before anything new. The response is the learning event. 03 Feedback · Closing the Gap
  • 💡 Feedback Must Cause Thinking Write "why might this be stronger with evidence?" not insert the evidence yourself. If feedback needs no effort from the learner, it is information, not feedback. Thinking is the point. 03 Feedback · Feedback & the Learner
  • 📊 Reduce Volume, Raise Focus A page of comments overwhelms. One or two precise targets land. Identify the one thing that would make the biggest difference right now and focus entirely on that. 03 Feedback · Timing & Frequency
  • ✏️ Comment Only Marking Remove grades from work in progress. Research showed comments alone produced the greatest gains. Adding a score to a comment cancels it out: students read the grade and ignore the feedback entirely. 02 Formative Assessment · Feedback That Moves Learning Forward

Technology second

Where technology fits

Audio comments and digital ink can shorten the gap between the work and your response to almost nothing, and feedback that arrives while the work is still fresh has a much better chance of shaping what pupils do next. The tool doesn't change the rule though, because a recorded comment nobody listens to is still marking rather than feedback. If you use the approved audio or annotation tool in your setting, plan the listening and the response with just as much care as the recording.
