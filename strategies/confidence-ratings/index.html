22 🎯Confidence Ratings

Dunlosky and Metcalfe showed students are most confident about things most recently read, not things they actually know. Rate confidence after each attempt, then reveal the answer. That gap is the target.

Suggested technology quiz tool with confidence rating · Informed by Dunlosky & Metcalfe

Find this under Check understanding

Part of Metacognition & Calibration in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Students are most confident about the things they read most recently, not the things they actually know. That is Dunlosky and Metcalfe's finding, and it explains a great deal of misdirected revision: recency masquerades as mastery, and decisions get made on the feeling.

Rating confidence after each retrieval attempt, then revealing the answer, turns that private feeling into something that can be checked. The gap between the rating and the result is the target.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

In a self-quizzing routine, pupils answer each question, write 1 to 5 for how sure they are, then check. One pupil finds her 5s are mostly right, but so are her 2s: she knows more than she feels she does, and her revision list changes accordingly.

Try it

A way to begin

Add a one-number confidence column to any answer-then-check activity the class already does. The overhead is seconds per question.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Systematic patterns: pupils always certain, pupils never certain. Both are calibration problems, and they need different conversations.
  • Whether the ratings change what pupils choose to revise. The number is only worth collecting if it changes a decision.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Rate after the attempt, not before it. The card is specific, because the attempt itself is evidence the rating should draw on.

Where this connects

  • 🔬 Teach the Illusion of Knowing Show students their own illusion of knowing. Ask them to rate confidence before revealing the answer. When they were certain and wrong, that is the precise moment to intervene. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • 📊 Retrieval Journals Zimmerman showed students who monitor their performance make better revision decisions. Log what came back, what did not, and what needs revisiting. It is a map of what to do next. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • ✅ Provide Instant Feedback Metcalfe shows instant feedback is essential for confident errors. For general retrieval, allow a processing gap before revealing answers. Speed matters most when confidence is highest. 01 Retrieval Practice · Metacognition & Calibration
  • 📊 Compare Predicted to Actual Winne: calibration improves when students see the gap between expected and real scores. Have them predict a mark, then compare, so monitoring is corrected by evidence over time. 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation · Self-Reflection & Evaluation
  • 💪 Build Realistic Confidence Zimmerman: self-efficacy drives effort, but it must be earned by past success, not pep talks. Point students to a recent task they mastered to set a credible expectation for this one. 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation · Forethought & Planning

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Dunlosky and Metcalfe's work on metacognitive monitoring is the direct source: judgements of learning track recency and fluency more faithfully than they track actual knowledge. The card’s attribution stands as published: Dunlosky & Metcalfe.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Retrieval Practice.
