Mini Whiteboards

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

08 ✍️Mini Whiteboards

All students write their response and hold it up simultaneously. The teacher reads the room in seconds: full participation, no waiting, no dominant voices. Works across all subjects.

Suggested technology multimodal response tools · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Check understanding · Increase participation · Make thinking visible · Support access and participation · Inclusive practice: access and participation · Try this tomorrow

Part of Elicit Evidence of Learning in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

The classic weakness of classroom questioning is that you hear from a handful of pupils and make assumptions about everyone else. Mini whiteboards deal with that directly, because every pupil answers, every answer is visible, and you can read the whole room in seconds.

They change who participates too. There's no waiting for a turn and no hiding behind the confident few, which is why this one strategy turns up in so many of the routes through this site.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A languages teacher checking verb endings asks the class to write the conjugation and hold their boards up together on a count of three. Thirty answers appear at once, two errors dominate, and so the next five minutes deal with those two errors rather than moving on.

Try it

A way to begin

Try one all-write, all-show question at tomorrow's key checkpoint. Showing together really matters, because staggered reveals let pupils copy the early boards.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • The answers from pupils you would never have called on, because that's the evidence you've been missing all along.
  • How quickly you can read the room, and what you do when what the room shows you disagrees with your plan.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Boards suit short-form answers, so for extended reasoning you'll need a different route to the same visibility.
  • The point is the evidence, not the equipment. If the routine drifts into a game of speed, the quality of the answers goes with it, and so does the diagnosis.

Technology second

Where technology fits

Multimodal response tools, through the approved tool in your setting, do the same job when you need typed answers, anonymity or a record, with every learner visible at once. The test is exactly the same either way, because a screen full of responses has done nothing until it changes what you do next.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Eliciting evidence from every pupil is one of Wiliam and Black's core formative assessment moves, and the whiteboard is simply the cheapest reliable way of doing it. The card’s attribution stands as published: Wiliam.
