04 📜Pick a Strategy on Purpose

Flavell: planning means selecting an approach, not defaulting to one. Have students name which method they will use for this problem and why, before the first line goes down.

Suggested technology strategy menu in a shared doc · Informed by Flavell

Find this under Develop metacognition · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Forethought & Planning in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Most pupils don't really choose a method at all, they just reach for the first one that comes to mind, or the one they always use. Flavell's point is that planning means selecting an approach rather than defaulting to one, and that selection only happens when there's a moment of deliberate choice.

Asking pupils to name which method they'll use for this problem, and why, before the first line goes down makes that choice visible, both to them and to you.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

A pair of simultaneous equations goes up, alongside a short menu of the methods the class has been taught, such as substitution, elimination and graphing. Each pupil writes the method they're going to use and one line of reasoning in the margin before they start. You scan the reasons rather than just the choices, because "the coefficients already match" is a really different answer from "it's the one I like".

Try it

A way to begin

On the next task with more than one sensible route through it, ask for the method and the why in the margin before anyone starts. It only costs thirty seconds.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pupils who pick the same method every time regardless of the problem, because that's habit rather than planning.
  • Whether the reasons they give mention features of the task itself. When they do, that's conditional knowledge starting to form.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Pupils can only select from strategies they've actually been taught, so if the menu is empty, teach the methods first and come back to this.
  • The why counts for more than the choice, and a defensible reason for the "wrong" method is actually better planning than a lucky default.

Where this connects

  • 🧰 Know When to Use Each Winne: conditional knowledge, knowing which strategy suits which task, is what separates skilled learners. Teach not just the method but the cues that signal when it is the right one. 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation · Strategy Knowledge & Use

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Flavell's metacognitive knowledge includes knowing your own strategies, and Winne adds the conditional layer on top of this, which is knowing which one this particular task calls for. The card’s attribution stands as published: Flavell.
