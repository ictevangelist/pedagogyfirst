Fade the Scaffolds

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

23 📉Fade the Scaffolds

Rosenshine: begin with full support, then fade. Show a complete example, then a partial one with gaps, then independent practice. Use fillable templates to structure the fading.

Suggested technology fillable template or shared doc · Informed by Rosenshine; Sweller

Find this under Make modelling clearer · Build independence · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand

Part of Modelling & Worked Examples in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Support that never fades teaches pupils to depend on the support, but support removed all at once tips them into overload. Rosenshine's resolution is a gradient, so a complete example first, then a partial one with gaps, then independent practice.

Each piece you remove hands one more part of the thinking over to the pupil, at a rate working memory can cope with, and that gradual handover is what all the scaffolding was for in the first place.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Your fillable template structures a science evaluation three times over, first fully modelled, then with the reasoning sentences gapped, then blank. The same document, with more taken out each time, takes the class from watching to doing.

Try it

A way to begin

Take one task you currently model in full and build the middle version, which is the same example with strategic gaps for pupils to complete.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which gaps get filled confidently and which ones bring the room to a stop, because the stopping points show you where the hard thinking actually is.
  • Who still reaches for the complete version, and how long they carry on doing so.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The rate of fading is the judgement, and it's made per class and sometimes per pupil. The strand prose says scaffolds are removed as competence grows, and competence is something you judge in the room.
  • Fading the visible scaffold pairs with fading your own live support, so the template is one strand of a wider handover.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Rosenshine's principles put scaffolding and its removal at the heart of instruction, and Sweller's load theory explains why that removal has to be gradual. The card’s attribution stands as published: Rosenshine; Sweller.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Explanations & Modelling.
