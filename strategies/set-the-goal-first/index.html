Set the Goal First

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 🎯Set the Goal First

Zimmerman: planning begins with a specific, proximal goal, not a vague aim. Have students state what a good answer to this question looks like before they start writing it.

Suggested technology shared success criteria on screen · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Develop metacognition · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Forethought & Planning in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

A vague aim doesn't really give a pupil anything to steer by. "Do your best on question three" isn't something they can check themselves against, and that's why Zimmerman puts a specific, proximal goal right at the start of the self-regulation cycle, so that learners look ahead before they act.

When you ask pupils to say what a good answer to this question would look like, before they start writing it, the aim becomes something they can check their work against while the work is still happening, and that's a really useful thing for them to have.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Before a history essay, pupils jot down three things a strong answer to this exact question would need to include. The class compares suggestions and agrees a shared version, and it stays up on the screen while they write. This means a pupil who pauses mid-paragraph now has something concrete to check themselves against.

Try it

A way to begin

Before your next extended answer, try asking "what would a good answer to this include?" and take suggestions before anyone writes a word. It takes two minutes and there's no preparation needed.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether the goals pupils come up with are specific, or just the task said back to you. "Write a good essay" is really just the vague aim in a slightly different form.
  • Whether anyone refers back to the goal while they're working. A goal that gets set and then ignored isn't really part of the cycle yet.
  • Whether the goal they've stated actually changes what gets written.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Pupils who are new to this will come up with woolly criteria at first, and that's a reason to model the move for them rather than a reason to give up on it.
  • The long-term aim is pupils who set the goal without being asked. The prompt is a scaffold, and like any scaffold it's there to be faded over time.
