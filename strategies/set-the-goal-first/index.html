Set the Goal First

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

01 🎯Set the Goal First

Zimmerman: planning begins with a specific, proximal goal, not a vague aim. Have students state what a good answer to this question looks like before they start writing it.

Suggested technology shared success criteria on screen · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Develop metacognition · Inclusive practice: independence and self regulation

Part of Forethought & Planning in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

A vague aim gives a pupil nothing to steer by. "Do your best on question three" cannot be monitored against, which is why Zimmerman puts a specific, proximal goal at the very start of the self-regulation cycle: learners look ahead before they act.

Asking pupils to state what a good answer to this question looks like, before they start writing it, turns the aim into something they can check their work against while the work is still happening.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Before a history essay, pupils jot down three things a strong answer to this exact question would contain. The class compares suggestions, agrees a shared version, and it stays on screen while they write. A pupil who pauses mid-paragraph now has something concrete to pause against.

Try it

A way to begin

Before your next extended answer, ask "what would a good answer to this include?" and take suggestions before anyone writes a word. Two minutes, no preparation.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether the goals pupils state are specific, or just the task restated. "Write a good essay" is the vague aim wearing a costume.
  • Whether anyone refers back to the goal mid-task. A goal set and then ignored has not yet entered the cycle.
  • Whether the stated goal changes what gets written.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Pupils new to this will produce woolly criteria at first. That is a reason to model the move, not to abandon it.
  • The long-term aim is pupils who set the goal without being asked. The prompt is scaffolding, and scaffolding is for fading.
