Clarity Over Novelty

04 💡Clarity Over Novelty

The EEF found clarity matters more than delivery method. A diagram under a visualiser can outperform a polished video. Choose the tool that makes the idea clearest, not the newest.

Suggested technology any display tool used with purpose · Informed by EEF

Find this under Explain difficult ideas · Use technology with greater purpose

Part of Structuring Explanations in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

There's a common assumption that the more sophisticated tool teaches better, but the EEF found otherwise. Clarity matters more than the delivery method, and a simple diagram under a visualiser can outperform a polished video.

So when it comes to any display, the question to ask is not so much whether it's impressive, but whether it makes this idea clearest for this class.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Planning to teach the structure of the heart, you weigh up a slick animation against drawing and labelling the diagram live under a visualiser, and choose the live drawing, because the class gets to see the structure assembled one part at a time, at the pace of the explanation.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils can put the idea back into their own words afterwards, whichever display you went with.
  • Whether the time spent producing a resource shows up in what the class understood, because effort and clarity aren't the same thing.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Clearest is a judgement about a particular idea and a particular class, and it's a judgement only the teacher in the room can make.
  • There's nothing wrong with a new tool as such, and the test is simply whether it makes the idea clearer than what you already do.
