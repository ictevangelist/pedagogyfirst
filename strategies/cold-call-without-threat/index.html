Cold Call Without Threat

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

11 🎲Cold Call Without Threat

Good and Brophy showed differential questioning reinforces attainment gaps. Direct questions are equitable only when the climate supports it. Norms: no wrong attempts, only thinking.

Suggested technology random selector or seating plan tool · Informed by Good & Brophy; Wiliam

Find this under Increase participation · Inclusive practice: access and participation

Part of Participation & Equity in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Good and Brophy showed that questioning is rarely spread evenly, because teachers direct more questions, and harder ones, to the students they expect more from, and the gap reinforces itself. Cold calling answers that problem, but only if the climate supports it.

Direct questions are only equitable when a wrong answer costs nothing, and the norm the card names carries the whole strategy: no wrong attempts, only thinking.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

A named pupil offers an answer that's wrong. You treat it as thinking to work with, ask what led them there, and later in the lesson come back to the same pupil with a related question, so they can see that being wrong didn't take them out of the conversation.

Try it

A way to begin

Establish the norm before you lean on it. Name it with the class, and hold the room to it the first time a wrong answer draws a reaction.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What actually happens after a wrong answer, both your response and the room's.
  • How your quieter pupils look when names are used, because you can usually tell the difference between a pupil who's relieved to be included and one who's dreading it.
  • Whether your harder questions still drift towards the same few students.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Climate is the precondition rather than an optional extra. If cold calling in this class would feel like exposure, the norms come first, and how fast to move with a particular anxious pupil is your call, because you know the class, and no guide can make it for you.
  • Hands down is the companion routine, and this card supplies the conditions that make it fair.

Where this connects

  • ✋ Hands Down Wiliam shows hand-raising distorts who answers. A small group volunteers repeatedly; the rest disengage. Remove hands and direct to named students. Every student should be asked. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Participation & Equity
  • 📊 Track Who You Ask Good and Brophy found teachers direct harder questions to higher-attaining students. Track participation across lessons. Patterns invisible in the moment become visible on paper. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Participation & Equity
  • 🎙️ Deliberate Cold Call Select who answers deliberately, not at random. Track participation so nobody stays invisible across lessons. Where appropriate, target the students whose thinking you most need to hear. 02 Formative Assessment · Elicit Evidence of Learning
  • ⏰ Wait Time 1: After You Ask Rowe found most teachers wait under one second before speaking. Extending to three seconds increases the length and complexity of responses. Silence is thinking, not a gap to fill. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Wait Time & Thinking Space
