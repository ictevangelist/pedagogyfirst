Check, Don’t Assume

05 ✅Check, Don’t Assume

Listening is not learning. Use live polls or mini whiteboards to check understanding at every step. Rosenshine: reteach before moving on. The explanation is the start, not the finish.

Suggested technology live polling or mini whiteboard tool · Informed by Rosenshine

Find this under Check understanding

Part of Structuring Explanations in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

A class that's listening politely looks exactly the same as a class that understands, but listening isn't learning, and an explanation that's never checked is really just a guess about thirty minds.

Rosenshine's answer is to check understanding at every step and to reteach before moving on, so the explanation is the start of the process rather than the finish.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Halfway through explaining photosynthesis, a teacher poses one question to the whole class and every pupil answers on a mini whiteboard. The spread of answers decides what happens next, so the class either moves on or the step that wobbled gets retaught.

Try it

A way to begin

Plan one check into the middle of your next explanation, and have everyone answer it at once rather than one volunteer.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • The gap between who looked like they were following and who actually showed you they were.
  • What the wrong answers have in common, because a shared error usually points at the explanation rather than the pupils.
  • How often the check changes what you do next. If it never does, it's become a ritual rather than a check.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Checking every step changes your pacing, and that's the point rather than a cost, because Rosenshine's sequence assumes you'll sometimes need to reteach.
  • Whole-class response works better than sampling one or two hands, because the pupils you don't hear from are the ones assumptions tend to get made about.
