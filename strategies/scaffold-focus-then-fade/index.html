Scaffold Focus, Then Fade

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

07 🔒Scaffold Focus, Then Fade

Zimmerman: monitoring needs a low-distraction environment first. Use classroom.cloud to scaffold focus while students are learning, then withdraw it as they manage attention themselves.

Suggested technology classroom.cloud · Informed by Zimmerman

Find this under Use technology with greater purpose

Part of Performance & Monitoring in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Monitoring your own attention is a skill in itself, and Zimmerman's point is that it develops best in a low-distraction environment to begin with. A pupil can't really practise noticing their focus slipping while twelve tabs are competing for it.

So you scaffold the environment to start with, and then withdraw the scaffold as pupils show they can manage their attention themselves. If the restriction never lifts, the tool is doing the regulating, and the capacity never gets built.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

During on-screen independent work, you use the school's classroom management tool to limit what can be open for the first few weeks of the routine. As pupils show they can hold their focus, you relax the limits, class by class or pupil by pupil, until managing the screen is their job rather than the software's.

Try it

A way to begin

Before you switch any restriction on, decide what the first step of switching it off will look like, and what evidence would tell you it's time.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What happens when a restriction lifts, because that first unrestricted session tells you whether focus was actually being learned or just enforced.
  • Pupils who only work while they know the tool is active. That's exactly the dependency the fading is designed to avoid.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Don't skip the fading. A monitoring tool that's used permanently takes away the very work that builds the capacity, and a pupil who always has the tool doing the regulating never gets to practise doing it themselves.
  • When to fade, and for whom, is a judgement call, and you know your class far better than any guide does.

Technology second

Where technology fits

The card names a classroom management tool, so use whichever one is approved in your setting, and hold it to the strand's rule, which is to scaffold the regulation and then fade the scaffold. The aim is a learner who no longer needs the tool.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Structuring the environment is one of the self-regulatory strategies Zimmerman documented in the performance phase, and the handover from external structure to self-management is where the learning actually sits. The card’s attribution stands as published: Zimmerman.
