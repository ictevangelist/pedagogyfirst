Make Thinking Visible

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

10 📝Make Thinking Visible

Flavell: regulation you cannot see, you cannot coach. Have students leave a brief trace of their reasoning as they work, so you and they can inspect the process, not just the product.

Suggested technology shared doc showing working e.g. OneNote · Informed by Flavell

Find this under Make thinking visible

Part of Performance & Monitoring in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Regulation you can't see is really hard to coach. A finished product tells you whether a pupil got there, but it says almost nothing about the route they took, and the route is where the regulation succeeds or falls down.

Flavell's work is the ground here, because pupils can become more aware of their own thinking, and a brief written trace of their reasoning is that awareness made into something you can actually look at, both you and them.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Working through a multi-step maths problem, pupils add a one-line note at each step saying why they took it. When you review the traces, you find a pupil whose method is sound but aimed at the wrong quantity from step one. The product was wrong, but the trace shows exactly which part of the process wasn't.

Try it

A way to begin

Ask for a single margin note on the next problem, along the lines of "why I chose this step". One line is plenty to start with.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • The mismatches, such as tidy products built on shaky reasoning and rough products built on sound reasoning. Both of these are invisible without the trace.
  • Traces written after the event to justify the answer. Explaining it afterwards is a really different activity from showing your working.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Keep the trace brief, because if writing about the thinking starts competing with the thinking itself, the scaffold has taken over from the task.
  • The trace is for looking at and coaching from, not for marking, so say that out loud, or pupils will write it for the grade.

Where this connects

  • 🔍 Questions That Make Thinking Visible Anderson and Krathwohl map six levels: remembering, understanding, applying, analysing, evaluating, creating. These are not a hierarchy of worth. Recall is foundational, not inferior. 04 Questioning & Discussion · The Quality of Questions
  • 🗣 Think Aloud Rosenshine: model the process, not the product. Use a visualiser or screen share to think aloud live. Narrate your decisions as you work. Make invisible expertise visible to all. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Modelling & Worked Examples
  • ❔ Ask Yourself As You Go Flavell: monitoring means questioning your understanding mid-task. Teach students to pause and ask “do I actually understand this?”, so gaps surface in time to fix them. 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation · Performance & Monitoring
  • 💬 Dialogic Feedback Build reply loops into feedback. Let students question and clarify comments over time rather than receive a final judgment. Carless showed uptake rises when feedback is a conversation. 03 Feedback · Source & Direction

Technology second

Where technology fits

A shared document in your setting's approved platform lets the trace build up where you can see it during the task, rather than after the books close, so you can act on what you're seeing while it still matters. The document shows you the process, but the coaching of it stays with you.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Flavell gave us the language for pupils' awareness of their own thinking, and the trace turns that awareness into something you can actually work with. The card’s attribution stands as published: Flavell.
