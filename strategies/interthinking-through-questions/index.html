Interthinking Through Questions

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

23 💬Interthinking Through Questions

Mercer’s interthinking describes shared cognition built through language. Student questions sustain joint enquiry. The goal is thinking in common, not performance for the teacher.

Suggested technology collaborative discussion or writing tool · Informed by Mercer

Find this under Improve classroom discussion

Part of Student-Generated Questions in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Interthinking is Mercer's word for something classrooms can be unusually good at, which is thinking genuinely shared, built between people through language. Student questions are what keep that joint enquiry moving along.

The distinction the card draws is the one to hold onto here: the goal is thinking in common, not performance for the teacher.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

A group is working out why their bridge design failed under load, and the questions that move the work along pass between the pupils, such as was it the joints or the span, and how do we test that without breaking the second one. Nothing routes through you, and the enquiry belongs to the group.

Try it

A way to begin

Sit yourself near one group during a task and count how many of the questions being asked are pupil-to-pupil rather than pupil-to-you.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether the questions route through you or pass across the group.
  • Whether a pupil's question ever changes the group's direction, because that's the joint enquiry actually working.
  • How the talk changes when you come near, because a group performing for you sounds different from a group that's genuinely thinking together.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Exploratory talk is the ground this grows in, so groups that haven't learned its norms will find it hard to interthink, whatever the task is.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🗣 Exploratory Talk Mercer distinguishes disputational, cumulative and exploratory talk. Only exploratory talk, where students challenge ideas with evidence and build on each thinking, improves outcomes. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 👥 Reciprocal Peer Questioning King’s reciprocal questioning has students generate and pose questions to each other, with neither knowing the answer. This produces genuine enquiry rather than rehearsal. Aim to think. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Student-Generated Questions
  • 📚 Ground Rules for Talk Mercer found teaching norms of exploratory talk transforms discussion. Norms co-constructed with the class outperform those displayed or modelled. Make the ground rules together. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Dialogic Teaching & Discussion
  • 📄 Questions That Drive Enquiry King found connecting questions to prior knowledge produces the most powerful learning. Ask students to write what they know and want to know. Their questions become the entry point. 04 Questioning & Discussion · Student-Generated Questions

Where the evidence sits
