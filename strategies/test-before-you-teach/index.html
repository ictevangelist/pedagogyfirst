Test Before You Teach

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

04 📈Test Before You Teach

Quiz students before teaching. Struggling to retrieve primes the brain; students who fail before instruction remember the answer more reliably once taught. Failure is productive.

Suggested technology pre-lesson quiz tool · Informed by Bjork; Kapur

Part of The Testing Effect in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Quizzing pupils on content you haven't taught them yet sounds backwards, but that is exactly what the card is describing. Struggling to retrieve primes the brain, and students who fail before instruction remember the answer more reliably once it's taught.

It's also a helpful way of thinking about failure, because here the wrong answers aren't a problem you have to manage. The struggle to answer is doing useful work before the teaching even starts.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Before a lesson introducing the causes of the First World War, you pose three questions the class can't yet answer well and ask for best guesses. There are no marks, and no discussion beyond a brief reveal. The lesson then teaches the material, and those early guesses give the new content something to attach itself to.

Try it

A way to begin

Try opening one new topic with two or three questions pupils can't yet answer, asked for best guesses, before any of the teaching happens. Keep it brief, because it's the priming you're after rather than the scores.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How pupils handle being asked about things they don't know yet. This only really works if guessing wrong is visibly safe in your classroom.
  • Whether the material you pre-tested comes back more reliably later on than material you introduced without the quiz.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • It's worth being explicit with the class about why you're doing it. Pupils who are told that failing here is productive respond really differently from pupils who suspect they're being caught out.
  • This pairs naturally with the generation effect, because a wrong answer generated before the right one is revealed is doing useful work for memory.

Where this connects

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Bjork's work on pretesting and Kapur's on productive failure point the same way, which is that unsuccessful retrieval before instruction can prepare memory for what comes next. The card’s attribution stands as published: Bjork; Kapur.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Retrieval Practice.
