Knowledge Comes First

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

02 🧠Knowledge Comes First

Willingham: you cannot plan or monitor a task you know nothing about. Teach the content first; metacognition works on knowledge, never instead of it. Build the base, then regulate.

Suggested technology retrieval starter to check the base · Informed by Willingham

Part of Forethought & Planning in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

There's a tempting version of metacognition where pupils are taught to plan, monitor and reflect as a free-standing skill. Willingham's point cuts right through it, because you simply can't plan or monitor a task you know nothing about, and a pupil can't judge whether their circuit explanation is going wrong if they don't know the circuit rules.

So the order really does matter here. Teach the content first, because metacognition works on knowledge, never instead of it.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Before a science extended task that asks pupils to plan and self-check their approach, you run a short retrieval starter on the underlying content. Pupils who can't yet retrieve the basics get more practice on those before they're asked to do the planning and monitoring steps, so the regulation is only ever pointed at material that's actually there to be regulated.

Try it

A way to begin

Put a retrieval starter in front of your next task that asks for planning or self-checking, and let the results tell you who's ready to regulate and who still needs more time with the content.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pupils whose reflections are vague or generic. Often the gap is in the knowledge rather than in the reflective skill.
  • Whether the quality of pupils' plans follows how secure their content is. It usually does, and once you've seen that pattern for yourself, the card's point rather makes itself.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • This does cut against bolt-on study skills lessons taught separately from subjects. Build the base first, then regulate, and do both inside the topic itself.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Willingham's wider argument is that thinking skills depend on knowledge of the thing being thought about, and the same warning against teaching metacognition in the abstract runs right through the strand's evidence base. The card’s attribution stands as published: Willingham.
