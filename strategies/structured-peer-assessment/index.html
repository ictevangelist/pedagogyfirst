Structured Peer Assessment

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

19 🔍Structured Peer Assessment

Peer feedback only works when students understand quality. Use exemplars first so students assess against shared criteria. Without shared criteria, peer feedback is opinion, not evidence.

Suggested technology peer assessment tool · Informed by Sadler, Berger

Part of Students as Learning Resources in 02 Formative Assessment, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Peer assessment has a bad name in classrooms where it means swapping books and hunting for spelling errors. The card locates the fault precisely: without shared criteria, peer feedback is opinion, not evidence.

The structure starts before any assessing happens. Pupils who have examined exemplars and agreed what quality looks like have a standard to assess against; pupils who have not can only compare the work with their own taste.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

Before assessing each other's speeches, the class watches two recorded examples and agrees what separated them. Only then do pairs assess, against that agreed list, with one rule: every judgement has to point to a specific moment in the speech.

Try it

A way to begin

Before your next peer assessment, insert one exemplar discussion and have the class agree the criteria they will use. Same task, one added step.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether feedback cites the shared criteria or drifts back to personal preference.
  • How pupils receive peer judgements compared with yours, and whether criteria they helped build change that.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Pairings carry social weight. Who assesses whom, and whether some work is better assessed anonymously, is exactly the kind of call the teacher in the room makes and a website cannot.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 📄 Exemplars Before Rubrics Show work at different standards before students start their own. Students spot quality in someone else's work more readily than in their own. A rubric tells students what quality is; an exemplar shows them. 02 Formative Assessment · Clarify Learning Intentions
  • 👥 Peer Feedback with Structure Give students criteria and a protocol before they assess each other's work. Topping showed peer feedback improves learning for both, but without scaffolding it becomes empty praise. 03 Feedback · Source & Direction
  • 🤝 Build Success Criteria Together Ask students what excellent looks like before they begin. Their language, not yours, becomes the standard. Criteria they articulate themselves land far more deeply than criteria handed to them. 02 Formative Assessment · Clarify Learning Intentions
  • 📜 Sadler's Three Conditions Share the criteria before students begin. Show them how to compare their work against it. Give them time to act on what they find. Without all three steps, feedback cannot function. 03 Feedback · What Feedback Is

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Sadler's argument is that any assessor needs a concept of quality they can apply; Berger's classroom practice shows pupils building that concept from real examples of excellent work. The card’s attribution stands as published: Sadler, Berger.

The fuller picture, sources included, is in further reading and evidence for Formative Assessment.
