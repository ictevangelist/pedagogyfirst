02 📋Low-Stakes Starter Quizzes

Five questions per lesson on prior content; nothing in the gradebook. Agarwal found routine quizzing reduces test anxiety rather than causing it. The quiz is not the assessment. It is the learning.

Suggested technology quiz or response tool · Informed by Roediger & Karpicke; Agarwal et al.

Find this under Help pupils remember more

Part of The Testing Effect in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Five questions at the start of the lesson, on prior content, with nothing going in the gradebook. The routine is unremarkable to watch, which is rather the point: it makes regular retrieval a habit instead of an event.

The stakes are doing real work here. Agarwal found routine quizzing reduces test anxiety rather than causing it; when quizzing is frequent and nothing rides on it, it stops being a threat and becomes practice. As the card says, the quiz is not the assessment. It is the learning.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A science class starts every lesson the same way: five questions on the board covering earlier topics, answered from memory in the back of books, self-marked in a minute. Nothing is collected and nothing is graded. The teacher notes which questions caused trouble and moves on.

Try it

A way to begin

Pick one class and run five questions tomorrow, self-marked. Reusing questions from earlier lessons is fine; frequency beats novelty.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which questions the class keeps getting wrong. Those topics need reteaching, not just re-quizzing.
  • Whether the routine stays low-stakes in feel as well as in fact. Agarwal's finding shows up as pupils treating the quiz as normal.
  • How long it actually takes. If the starter is swallowing fifteen minutes, trim the admin, not the retrieval.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Keeping it out of the gradebook is not an optional nicety; the low stakes are what make the anxiety finding hold.
  • Self-marking keeps the workload at zero and puts the checking where it is most useful, in the pupil's hands, immediately.

Where this connects

  • 🔄 Yesterday, Last Week, Last Month Three questions per lesson: one from yesterday, one from last week, one from last month. These intervals are a starting point; complex topics may need revisiting more frequently. 01 Retrieval Practice · Spacing & Interleaving
  • ✍️ Brain Dump A blank page, two minutes, no notes: write everything you remember. The imperfect attempt is the point. What students cannot recall shows precisely where teaching needs to return. 01 Retrieval Practice · The Testing Effect

Technology second

Where technology fits

This is the routine where an approved quiz or response tool in your setting can genuinely help: quick two-minute starters every lesson with no marking, and results you can glance at while pupils move on. Let the tool manage the admin, the collecting and the scoring, and keep the retrieval effort with the pupils. If it starts to feel like a fastest finger first round, rewarding speed over thought, it is removing the difficulty that makes the practice worthwhile.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Roediger and Karpicke supply the memory case for frequent testing; Agarwal's classroom studies supply the reassurance that, done routinely and without stakes, it reduces test anxiety rather than feeding it. The card’s attribution stands as published: Roediger & Karpicke; Agarwal et al..
