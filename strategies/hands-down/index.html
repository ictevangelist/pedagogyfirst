Hands Down

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

10 ✋Hands Down

Wiliam shows hand-raising distorts who answers. A small group volunteers repeatedly; the rest disengage. Remove hands and direct to named students. Every student should be asked.

Suggested technology random name picker or class list tool · Informed by Wiliam

Find this under Increase participation · Inclusive practice: access and participation · Try this tomorrow

Part of Participation & Equity in 04 Questioning & Discussion, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Hand-raising looks like participation, but Wiliam's point is that it actually distorts it, because a small group volunteers over and over again and the rest learn that waiting patiently works. The pupils you most need to hear from are usually not the ones with their hands up.

Removing hands and directing questions to named pupils changes the contract, because every pupil now owns the possibility of being asked, and that changes how they listen.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

A teacher tells the class the new normal, which is hands down unless they're asked for, and the teacher will be choosing who answers. Questions go to named pupils across the fortnight, tracked loosely on a class list so nobody stays invisible. The volunteers grumble for a week or so, but the room's attention changes within days.

Try it

A way to begin

Try it with one class for one week, hands down. Pair it with a visible norm that a wrong attempt is thinking rather than failure, or your quiet pupils will experience it as exposure rather than inclusion.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How the listening changes, because when anyone might be asked, the question belongs to everyone.
  • Your own selection pattern, because tracking who you ask, even roughly, tends to reveal habits you didn't know you had.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Climate comes first, and the card on cold calling without threat is the necessary companion, because direct questioning is only fair when the room is safe for wrong answers.
  • Hands still have a place for genuine volunteering, for questions to you, and for contributions you haven't asked for. What changes is who controls how the answering gets shared out.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Good and Brophy's classroom studies documented how questioning patterns can reinforce attainment gaps without anyone noticing, and that's the problem this routine exists to interrupt. The card’s attribution stands as published: Wiliam.
