During, Not Just After

Exactly as it appears on the card

08 🔄During, Not Just After

Feedback given while students are still working is formative. Feedback returned weeks later is a post-mortem. Move around the room, intervene in the moment. Proximity is strategy.

Suggested technology live collaboration or monitoring tool · Informed by Wiliam; Black & Wiliam

Part of Timing & Frequency in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Feedback returned weeks after the work is a post-mortem: accurate, perhaps, but too late to change anything. Feedback given while pupils are still working shapes the work itself, which is the formative case Black and Wiliam made.

Proximity is strategy. Moving around the room during the task puts you where feedback is cheapest and most usable: at the point of the error, in the moment it happens.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

While pupils draft persuasive letters, the teacher circulates on a deliberate route rather than answering hands. Reading over shoulders, she catches the same weak opening in four books and pauses the room for ninety seconds to address it before it spreads further.

Try it

A way to begin

In your next extended task, plan your route around the room the way you would plan a question: who you will reach first, and what you are looking for when you arrive.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • How many errors you catch mid-task that would otherwise have travelled home in thirty books.
  • Which pupils you naturally reach and which you consistently miss. A deliberate route usually exposes a pattern.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Intervening in the moment suits some errors and not others; on complex thinking the guide advises letting the struggle run first. In-the-moment feedback is a timing choice, not a default.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

This is Black and Wiliam's central argument in practical form: assessment is formative only when it adjusts teaching and learning while the learning is still underway. The card’s attribution stands as published: Wiliam; Black & Wiliam.
