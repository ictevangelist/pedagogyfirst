Say It or Show It, Not Both

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

09 🔇Say It or Show It, Not Both

Sweller’s redundancy effect: narrating text on screen forces double processing. Say it or show it, not both. Use the B or W key to blank the slide while you talk through the idea.

Suggested technology B/W key in PowerPoint or Keynote · Informed by Sweller

Find this under Reduce unnecessary cognitive demand · Inclusive practice: working memory and cognitive demand · Try this tomorrow

Part of Managing Cognitive Load in 05 Explanations & Modelling, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Reading text aloud while the same text sits on the screen feels thorough, but Sweller's redundancy effect says it's the opposite. Pupils end up processing the written and spoken versions at once, the two streams compete with each other, and working memory ends up carrying both.

The fix costs nothing at all. Say it, or show it, and stop doing both at the same time. Of all the things in this strand that lighten the load on working memory, this is probably the easiest one to do tomorrow.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Mid-explanation, you press B, the screen goes black, thirty pairs of eyes come up, and the idea gets explained in speech with nothing competing for attention. When the diagram is needed the screen comes back, and you talk over the image rather than reading beside it.

Try it

A way to begin

Take one slide you'll teach this week, delete the sentences and keep the image, and say the deleted words instead. The B key, or W for a white screen, does the same job mid-flow.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Where pupils look when the screen is blank, compared with when it's full of text.
  • What your checks for understanding tell you afterwards. A cleaner explanation should show up as cleaner recall, and if it doesn't, that's worth knowing too.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • The effect is about doing both at the same time. Text pupils need to read silently, in their own time, is a different situation and a perfectly legitimate one.
  • A blank screen puts all the weight on your spoken explanation, which is exactly why this pairs so well with small steps and the other load principles in the strand.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🧠 Four Slots, No More Sweller: working memory holds about four items. Every screen element competes for those slots. Strip slides to essentials. Press B to blank the screen when you want attention on you. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • ❌ Remove the Clutter Mayer’s coherence: remove every word, image and sound that does not support learning. Strip clip art from slides. Cut background music. Every element on screen must earn its place. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Managing Cognitive Load
  • 🎧 Narrate, Don’t Label Mayer’s modality: people learn better from images with narration than images with text. Record a narrated screencast over a diagram. Let the voice do what text would overload. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
  • 🖼️ Words and Images Together Paivio’s dual coding: the brain processes words and images through separate channels. Design every slide with both: a key visual paired with minimal text. Two routes to the same idea. 05 Explanations & Modelling · Multimedia & Dual Coding
