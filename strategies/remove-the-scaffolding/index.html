Remove the Scaffolding

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

12 🚫Remove the Scaffolding

Retrieval with notes open is reading, not retrieval. Fade support gradually: notes first, then headings only, then nothing. The transition to unaided retrieval is where consolidation happens.

Suggested technology quiz tool; timed or closed-book mode · Informed by R. Bjork; Kornell

Part of Desirable Difficulties in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Retrieval with notes open is reading, not retrieval. The card's line is worth keeping whole, because it describes something that happens in plenty of well-intentioned classrooms, where the activity looks like a quiz but the notes on the desk are doing the remembering.

The answer isn't to ban support. It's to fade it on a schedule, so notes first, then headings only, then nothing at all. The move to unaided retrieval is where the memory gets its strengthening, so the fade deserves as much planning as the support did.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Reviewing a topic across three lessons, you run the same retrieval task each time with a little less on the table, so full notes in the first lesson, a five-heading skeleton in the second, and a blank page in the third. The questions stay the same all the way through. It's only the support that shrinks.

Try it

A way to begin

Take a retrieval activity your class currently does with the notes open, and run it next time with headings only. One step of the fade is plenty to start with, and you can see how it goes from there.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • What happens at each step down. A sharp drop between the notes and the headings tells you the notes were doing the remembering, which is useful to know.
  • Who freezes when the last support goes. Cued recall is a gentler step back for those pupils, so nobody has to jump straight to the blank page.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • How fast to fade is a judgement made in the room. The teacher who knows the class can see whether the struggle is still productive, and no schedule on a website can make that call for you.
  • Support removed too early produces confusion rather than desirable difficulty. The struggle has to be in the retrieving, not in guessing what's wanted.

Technology second

Where technology fits

If you use an approved quiz tool for retrieval, its settings are part of the fade too, so hints off, closed-book or timed mode on, and anything that supplies the answer at the first hesitation switched off. Let the tool run the activity for you, but keep the struggle with the pupils.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Bjork's desirable difficulties frame the case for withdrawing support, and Kornell's work with him on how learners misread easy practice explains why the faded version feels worse than it really is. The card’s attribution stands as published: R. Bjork; Kornell.
