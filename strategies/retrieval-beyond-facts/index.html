Retrieval Beyond Facts

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

16 💬Retrieval Beyond Facts

Factual retrieval builds the schema; higher-order retrieval tests whether students can use it. Secure the foundations first, then push beyond them. Retrieval is not a pub quiz.

Suggested technology quiz tool with varied question types · Informed by Agarwal

Part of Retrieval Formats in 01 Retrieval Practice, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Retrieval is not a pub quiz. The card's warning is aimed at a real drift, where retrieval routines settle permanently at the level of dates, definitions and labelled diagrams, simply because those questions are the easiest ones to write and mark.

Agarwal's work behind this card cuts both ways. Factual retrieval builds the schema, so it isn't beneath anyone, and higher-order retrieval then tests whether students can actually use what all those facts add up to. Secure the foundations first, then push beyond them.

What it might look like

One version of it, in a classroom

After several factual quizzes on the causes of the Wall Street Crash, a history teacher's retrieval question changes shape, and pupils are asked which cause mattered most, argued from memory in five sentences. The facts they've been retrieving for weeks become the material for a judgement.

Try it

A way to begin

Audit a week of your retrieval questions against one simple test: could pupils answer these without understanding the topic? Then add one question that asks them to use the knowledge rather than just produce it.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Pupils who are fluent in the factual rounds but stall at application. The knowledge may well be there, just unpractised in use.
  • What your question mix has drifted towards over a term. It's worth actually checking rather than assuming.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Sequence really matters here. Higher-order retrieval on insecure foundations produces guessing rather than thinking, so the card's order, foundations first, is doing a lot of the work.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 🗺 Concept Mapping from Memory Karpicke and Blunt found concept mapping from memory outperformed re-reading for retention. Not just what students know but how ideas connect. Relational knowledge beats isolated facts. 01 Retrieval Practice · Retrieval Formats
  • 👥 Retrieval Through Teaching The learning happens during preparation, not delivery. Preparing to teach demands complete, organised retrieval. Organising knowledge to explain it is where consolidation occurs. 01 Retrieval Practice · Retrieval Formats
  • 📋 Low-Stakes Starter Quizzes Five questions per lesson on prior content; nothing in the gradebook. Agarwal found routine quizzing reduces test anxiety rather than causing it. The quiz is not the assessment. It is the learning. 01 Retrieval Practice · The Testing Effect
  • 📈 Surface, Deep and Transfer Hattie and Zierer distinguish surface (what), deep (why) and transfer questions. Each serves a different stage. Reaching transfer before surface is secure sets students up to fail. 04 Questioning & Discussion · The Quality of Questions

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Agarwal's studies of factual and higher-order quizzing sit directly behind this card, and they belong to the testing effect tradition that runs from Roediger and Karpicke onwards. The card’s attribution stands as published: Agarwal.
