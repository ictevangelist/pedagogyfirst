Judge Against a Standard

The strategy, as published

Exactly as it appears on the card

11 📏Judge Against a Standard

Zimmerman: self-judgement needs a reference, not a hunch. Give students the mark scheme or an exemplar to compare their work against, so evaluation is anchored to a real standard.

Suggested technology exemplar shown beside own work · Informed by Zimmerman

Part of Self-Reflection & Evaluation in 06 Metacognition & Self-Regulation, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

When you ask pupils to check their work, most of them check how it feels, and "it feels done" tends to win. Zimmerman's point is that self-judgement needs a reference rather than a hunch, because without a standard to compare against, pupils are really just reporting their mood.

Giving pupils the mark scheme or an exemplar to compare against turns "is it good?" into "does it do what this does?", and that's a question they can actually answer.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

Pupils lay their own paragraph beside an exemplar, with the mark scheme between them, and identify one criterion their work meets and one it misses. The judgement is a comparison with something real, and the missed criterion becomes the obvious next thing to work on.

Try it

A way to begin

In your next self-assessment moment, swap "check your work" for "compare your work to this", and put the this in front of them.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Whether pupils' judgements of their own work drift closer to yours over time, because calibration against a standard is something they can learn.
  • Pupils who can recite the criteria but can't find them in their own writing. Reading an exemplar is a skill in itself, and it needs teaching too.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Choose the exemplar to show the standard rather than perfection. Work that's just above the pupil's current level gives the comparison something to hold on to.
  • This pairs really naturally with goals set before the task, because the standard used at the end means more when it was in view at the start.

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Zimmerman's self-reflection phase turns on comparing performance against a standard, and this strategy simply makes sure the standard is real and present rather than imagined. The card’s attribution stands as published: Zimmerman.
