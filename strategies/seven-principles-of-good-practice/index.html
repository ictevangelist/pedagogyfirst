Seven Principles of Good Practice

Exactly as it appears on the card

20 💪Seven Principles of Good Practice

Audit your practice against Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's seven principles. Do you clarify criteria? Enable self-assessment? Encourage dialogue? Close the gap? Redesign where you do not.

Suggested technology feedback design checklist tool · Informed by Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick

Part of Feedback & the Learner in 03 Feedback, where it sits alongside its neighbouring strategies, the infographic and the thinking for the whole area.

Why you might use it

The problem it may help with

Sometimes the most useful move is an audit rather than a new strategy. Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's seven principles give you the checklist, so do you clarify criteria, enable self-assessment, encourage dialogue and help close the gap? The card's instruction is a direct one: redesign where you do not.

The card names four of the principles as its worked examples, but the full seven are worth having to hand for the audit. Good feedback practice clarifies what good performance looks like, develops self-assessment and reflection, delivers high quality information to pupils about their learning, encourages dialogue around learning with teachers and peers, encourages positive motivational beliefs, provides opportunities to close the gap between current and desired performance, and gives teachers information they can use to shape their teaching.

Their deeper argument, which gets picked up throughout this strand, is that feedback belongs inside self-regulation, and its job is to make pupils better at judging and improving their own work rather than more dependent on yours.

What it could look like

One way in your classroom

You audit one unit against the principles and find two things missing, because pupils never self-assess before submitting and no comment ever gets a reply. You change only those two things for the next unit and leave what already works alone.

What to notice

Reading what happens

  • Which principles your practice already covers without you ever having named them, and which are missing entirely.
  • Whether the redesigns hold once the unit is underway, or fall away under time pressure, because what falls first shows you what needs protecting.

Things to think about

Before and while you use it

  • Trying to audit everything at once tends to change nothing, so one unit looked at honestly will do far more than a whole curriculum skimmed.

Related strategies

Where this connects

  • 📜 Sadler's Three Conditions Share the criteria before students begin. Show them how to compare their work against it. Give them time to act on what they find. Without all three steps, feedback cannot function. 03 Feedback · What Feedback Is
  • 🔬 Self-Assessment as Process Ask students to assess their work against criteria before you mark it. Andrade found the act of comparing, not the accuracy of the judgment, improves performance. Process is the point. 03 Feedback · Source & Direction
  • 💬 Dialogic Feedback Build reply loops into feedback. Let students question and clarify comments over time rather than receive a final judgment. Carless showed uptake rises when feedback is a conversation. 03 Feedback · Source & Direction
  • 🛠️ Feedback as Design, Not Event Plan feedback into your scheme of work: who gives it, when, in what form, and when students act on it. Feedback designed into learning from the start works. Left to chance, it does not. 03 Feedback · Closing the Gap

The wider thinking

Where the evidence sits

Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick built on Sadler's foundations, reframing feedback around the pupil's growing power of self-regulation, and the seven principles are that argument in a form you can actually use. The card’s attribution stands as published: Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick.
